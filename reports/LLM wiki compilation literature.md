# Feedback loops now define LLM-wiki research

"LLM wiki" became a research field in 2026. Karpathy's April gist named the pattern: an LLM compiles raw sources into interlinked markdown pages and maintains them with ingest, query and lint passes ([Karpathy](https://gist.github.com/karpathy/442a6bf555914893e9891c11519de94f)). By November about a dozen arXiv preprints build or evaluate it, and none has a peer-reviewed version yet. The state of the art splits two ways. One branch is **agentic wikis that a strong model browses with tools**: LLM-Wiki with its Error Book, WikiLoop and DeepRefine with RL-trained edits, and Cochran's preregistered comparisons. The other is **budgeted wikis read in full by a fixed reader**, and WiCER and its streaming sequel are the only papers there ([WiCER](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.07068), [Streaming KC](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.09877)). WiCER's specific contribution is the combination of a small reader with the whole wiki in context or KV cache, omission as the main failure, and a CEGAR-style probe-repair loop. No other paper occupies that cell. Three bodies of evidence bear directly on WiCER's known problems. First, every independent comparison finds that **abstracted stores lose detail-level facts** unless they keep a path back to verbatim text: about 34% of answer entities never reach an LLM-built knowledge graph ([Han et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.11371)), and A-MEM's own authors moved to immutable verbatim storage ([FlexA-Mem](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.33274)). Second, **question-supervised curation generalizes only as far as question overlap**: gains run from +0.294 F1 on trained questions to zero on unrelated ones ([Pan & Yu](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.21829)). That is WiCER's probe vs held-out gap, reproduced independently. Third, **evaluation in this field is fragile**. Judge agreement is ρ = 0.04 on holistic criteria, and one paper's headline citation advantage disappeared after a scorer bug was fixed ([Cochran](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.18490)). The practical consequences for WiCER are a guard set and delta edits in the repair loop, coverage-controlled probes, a verbatim layer under any abstraction, a question-free keyfact-recall metric, and compile seeds treated as a random effect.

**Verification markers.** The research notes used several conventions, and this report maps them to three. **[V]**: the arXiv abstract, paper HTML or primary page was opened during research. Where a note says "abstract only", numbers beyond the abstract are not confirmed. **[S]**: secondhand, meaning a search snippet, blog, aggregator or third-party summary; treat any number as unconfirmed. **[M]**: from background memory, not re-checked. One conflict was resolved in favour of the more thorough check. Two notes marked Karpathy's gist as secondhand, but the wiki-generation note opened it, so it is [V] here. Facts about WiCER's own internal runs (probe-recovery rates, pinned/unpinned displacement, the TACT +0.56 render result) come from the project's measurements, not from the literature, and are labelled as such. The earlier context-compression review covers prompt compression, KV-cache quantization, question-guided sentence selection and abstention in depth. This report touches those topics only where the wiki literature changes the picture.

## Twelve 2026 preprints, and only WiCER serves a small in-context reader

The 2026 cluster is easier to read on two axes: **how the reader gets at the wiki** (whole wiki in context, or an agent navigating pages with tools) and **whether a feedback signal edits the wiki** (none, probe-driven pinning, or RL with a downstream reward). Most papers sit in the "agent with tools" column, because Karpathy's design assumes a capable model that reads index.md and follows links. The gist itself restricts the index approach to "~100 sources, ~hundreds of pages" and contains no evaluation ([Karpathy](https://gist.github.com/karpathy/442a6bf555914893e9891c11519de94f)) [V].

Three readings follow from the map. First, **WiCER is alone in the small in-context reader cell**. Cochran's 21× query-token overhead and the ignored index belong to agentic browsing. WiCER's KV-cache serving removes the per-query navigation cost, and that is the clearest differentiator to state in related work. Second, **the feedback-loop systems converge on the same safeguards**: a guard set against regressions (WikiLoop), credit only for improvement over the previous artifact (DeepRefine's Gain-Beyond-Draft), and a check on a held-out reader (WikiLoop's held-out Navigator). WiCER's loop has none of these yet. Third, **the mixed RAG-vs-wiki results are not contradictory**. Wright finds single-fact parity on course concepts, while WiCER finds 53–60% catastrophic failures on blind compilation of RepLiQA's detail questions. The differences are corpus size relative to wiki budget, compiler strength and question granularity, and the paper should say so explicitly.

The older lineage of article generators, STORM → Co-STORM → OmniThink → WikiAutoGen → deep-research agents, writes for human readers and measures outline recall, breadth and citation support. **None of it asks whether a downstream reader can answer detail questions from the article.** STORM's own evaluation still finds about 15% of sentences unsupported by their citations. Its top error categories are "improper inferential linking" and "inaccurate paraphrasing", and editors judged its articles less informative than revised Wikipedia ([STORM](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.14207v2)) [V]. The 2026 benchmarks point to the same weakness from the coverage side. Wiki Live Challenge scores deep-research agents against 100 recent Good Articles with 39 criteria and finds a significant gap ([Wiki Live](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.01590)) [V]. A third-party summary puts the best agent at about 30.8% fact coverage ([review](https://www.themoonlight.io/review/wiki-live-challenge-challenging-deep-research-agents-with-expert-level-wikipedia-articles)) [S]. LLMpedia reportedly attributes the open-ended gap to coverage more than hallucination ([LLMpedia](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.24080)) [S]. A useful framing for WiCER's related-work section treats compilation errors as two-sided. Errors of **commission** (unsupported or over-linked claims) are what the article-generation literature measures, through citation precision. Errors of **omission** (dropped details) are what WiCER measures. No paper measures both on the same artifact.

## Every abstracted store loses details unless verbatim text stays reachable

The strongest finding across these notes is that the same failure appears in three neighbouring literatures: graph and summary indexes, agent memory, and long-document summarization. **A query-agnostic LLM abstraction of a corpus drops the specific facts that later questions hinge on.** The systems that avoided the failure all kept raw text reachable.

In structured indexing, the controlled 2025 comparisons agree. Han et al. found that about **34% of answer entities never appear in the LLM-built knowledge graph** on both HotpotQA and NQ. Triples-only retrieval reaches 39.2% / 32.2% accuracy against RAG's 88.6% / 86.7%. On NovelQA detail questions, community-summary "global" GraphRAG scores **30.89 against RAG's 55.28**. On null questions it collapses from RAG's **96.01 to 19.27** ([Han et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.11371)) [V]. GraphRAG-Bench opens with "GraphRAG frequently underperforms vanilla RAG on many real-world tasks". On fact retrieval, graphs do not help (60.92 vs 60.14), while on contextual summarization they add about 13 points ([Xiang et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.05690)) [V]. A debiased re-evaluation finds GraphRAG gains "much more moderate than reported previously" ([Zeng et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.06331)) [V]. HippoRAG 2 concedes that structure-augmented RAG drops "considerably below standard RAG" on basic factual memory, and fixes it by making passages graph nodes ([HippoRAG 2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.14802)) [V]. RAPTOR keeps leaf chunks retrievable under its summary tree ([RAPTOR](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.18059)) [V]. KAG indexes the KG and the original chunks against each other ([KAG](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.13731)) [V]. ReadAgent's gist-only baseline loses to gist plus lookup of the original passage ([ReadAgent](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.09727)) [V]. The field's dividing line is now explicit: **local, detail and unanswerable questions favour raw chunks; global, multi-hop, temporal and comparison questions favour structure.** RepLiQA is mostly the first kind.

Agent-memory research reached the same conclusion from the other direction, and moved toward verbatim storage in 2026. LongMemEval found that replacing sessions with extracted facts or summaries as *values* "generally hurt QA because of information loss". Extracted facts helped only as *keys* (K = V + fact), adding +9.4% recall@k and +5.4% accuracy ([LongMemEval](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.10813)) [V]. On MemoryAgentBench accurate retrieval, BM25 over raw text scores 60.5 against **Mem0 32.6, Cognee 28.3, Zep 37.5 and GraphRAG 40.9** ([MemoryAgentBench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.05257)) [V]. TierMem names the "**write-before-query barrier**": summaries drop details a later query needs, and raw-only storage still wins (0.873 vs 0.851 on LoCoMo) ([TierMem](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.17913)) [V]. FlexA-Mem, from A-MEM's and ACE's authors, states that "summaries drop exact strings" and that memories built on LLM-written text "fall far behind at tight budgets". Its fix is to write each unit once into an immutable store and render a budgeted view per call ([FlexA-Mem](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.33274)) [V]. ACE names the dynamics of repeated rewriting: "**brevity bias**" drops insights in favour of concision, and "**context collapse**" lets iterative full rewrites erode details. Structured incremental deltas prevent both ([ACE](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.04618)) [V]. The summarization literature had already measured the same trade-off: incremental updating keeps more detail than hierarchical merging, at some cost in coherence ([BooookScore](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.00785)) [V].

The counter-evidence is narrower than it looks. Memori gets 81.95% on LoCoMo from compact triples plus summaries at 5% of full-context tokens ([Memori](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.19935)) [V]. Schema-Grounded Memory argues for an exact system of record with validation gates ([Petrov et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.27906)) [V]. Both are vendor or production papers, and LoCoMo numbers across vendor papers disagree by 20–30 points on the same split ([True Memory](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.04897), [Letta](https://www.letta.com/blog/benchmarking-ai-agent-memory) [S]). A 2026 study also shows that whether the scorer credits the raw, source or canonical form of a memory **flips orderings for Mem0 and MemoryOS** ([Panthi & Abdelfattah](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.24060)) [V]. Li et al. find that summary-based retrieval matches long context ([Li et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.01880)) [V], but those summaries are retrieved alongside the leaves, not instead of them.

For WiCER, this changes how blind compilation's collapse should be described. It is not a quirk of Haiku. It is the expected signature of a query-agnostic abstraction with **no leaf layer**: WiCER's wiki has to *be* the leaf layer for every fact that might be asked. The project's measured held-out failures are detail-level rather than document-level, and verbatim rendering beat truncation by +0.56 in the TACT runs (both internal measurements). Both fit the literature's prediction exactly. Probe-guided pinning amounts to re-inserting leaves one at a time.

## Repair generalizes only as far as probe coverage reaches

WiCER's central open problem is that repairs which fix probed facts do not carry over to held-out questions. The project measured pinned documents gaining +0.10 and unpinned documents losing −0.13. The 2026 literature has now reproduced this pattern and named its causes, though it has not solved it.

The cleanest outside measurement is Pan & Yu. They treat a knowledge store as a non-parametric model trained on (question, answer) pairs. Gains are **+0.294 F1 on the trained questions, +0.176 when a held-out question shares both keys with training, and parity when it shares none**. "Accuracy rises steadily with the share of the corpus the indexes cover" ([Pan & Yu](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.21829)) [V]. Self-evolving agent studies show the dynamic side. In SEAGym, "frequent updates may fail to improve held-out performance, useful intermediate snapshots may collapse later" ([SEAGym](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.17546)) [V]. Automated harness optimization finds benchmark-wide shortcuts that task holdout alone does not catch ([CHASE](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.18366)) [V]. Sleep-time compute, which is what WiCER's compile step amounts to, pays off in proportion to **how predictable the queries are from the context** ([Sleep-time Compute](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.13171)) [V]. A small probe set that does not resemble the real query distribution should therefore transfer poorly.

The successful "compile with self-generated questions" methods share one trait: they generate questions **broadly and in volume** rather than as a small diagnostic set. Cartridges trains a per-corpus KV cache from synthetic conversations about the whole corpus and matches in-context learning at 38.6× less memory ([Cartridges](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.06266)) [V]. EntiGraph expands a 1.3M-token corpus to 455M synthetic tokens, and accuracy scales log-linearly with that volume ([EntiGraph](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.07431)) [V]. Active Reading's self-generated study strategies lift unseen SimpleQA-Wiki questions to 66% ([Active Reading](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.09494)) [V]. Memory-R1 learns a useful write policy from only 152 QA pairs ([Memory-R1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.19828)) [V], which shows that a few questions can carry signal about *how* to write. It does not show they carry signal about *what* else to keep. Generated queries also need filtering: Doc2Query expansions hallucinate, and filtering them gives up to +16% effectiveness ([Doc2Query--](https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.03266)) [V].

Three more results constrain the repair loop. Without an external signal, self-correction fails and can degrade performance ([Huang et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.01798)) [V]. WiCER checks probes against the source corpus, which keeps it on the right side of that line; the weak link is the judge. In a fixed budget, pinning is **zero-sum**. WikiLoop's guard penalty exists to price exactly the displacement WiCER measured, and its edits still help a held-out Navigator ([WikiLoop](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.26604)) [V]. Finally, recompiling from scratch each iteration is the design ACE identifies as collapse-prone ([ACE](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.04618)) [V]. Each recompilation also draws fresh compile noise, so WiCER's per-iteration effects are measured against a moving baseline. Itemized delta edits address both problems at once. The earlier review adds one caution here: on MultiRC, sentences selected for current questions do no better than random deletion on later questions ([Yao et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.27301)) [V]. Any repair-loop result therefore needs an equal-budget random baseline.

## Judges, scorers and compile seeds can each flip a conclusion

The LLM-wiki preprints are small: 13 to 59 questions, single corpora and LLM judges. Their most transferable lessons are about measurement. Cochran's preregistered study found Spearman **ρ = 0.04 between judges on holistic groundedness and 0.81 on the most concrete criterion**. In v3 a scorer bug that truncated RAG evidence to 1,500 characters was fixed and checked against two human annotators, and **the wiki's citation advantage disappeared** ([Cochran](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.18490)) [V]. Huerta's own streaming paper reports "a pervasive LLM-as-judge confound on post-training knowledge" and switches to regret as the metric ([Streaming KC](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.09877)) [V]. That bears directly on WiCER's 1–5 judge scale.

The summarization-evaluation literature explains why judges struggle with a paraphrasing compiler. Re-evaluating five factuality metrics on eleven datasets, Godbole & Jia find them **biased against highly paraphrased outputs and against content drawn from far-away parts of the source** ([Godbole & Jia](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.14883)) [V]. In FABLES, no LLM rater correlates strongly with humans on unfaithful claims in book summaries, and summaries over-weight the ends of books ([FABLES](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.01261)) [V]. MSumBench finds systematic bias when LLMs evaluate their own summaries ([MSumBench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.00549)) [V], which matters when Haiku compiles and a Claude-family model judges. On topic-focused summaries, binary LLM judges including GPT-4 lost to specialized non-LLM metrics ([TofuEval](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.13249)) [V]. FACTS Grounding averages several judge families for this reason ([FACTS](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.03200)) [V].

The literature also supplies metrics that do not need questions at all. FineSurE's **completeness**, the share of source keyfacts that align to summary sentences, is the most direct measure of WiCER's omissions ([FineSurE](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.00908)) [V]. SummHay provides exact gold insights, so coverage can be scored exactly ([SummHay](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.01370)) [V]. MiniCheck-FT5 matches GPT-4 at grounding checks for about 400× lower cost ([MiniCheck](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.10774)) [V]. VeriTrail traces where unsupported content entered a multi-step pipeline such as GraphRAG ([VeriTrail](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.21786)) [V]. Its framing separates compile-time loss (the fact is absent or altered in the wiki) from read-time failure (the fact is present but the reader misses it). The Sufficient Context autorater answers "does this context contain the answer?" ([Joren et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.06037)) [V]. No paper defines a standard omission metric for corpus-to-wiki compilation, so WiCER could set one.

On statistics, Miller shows clustered standard errors can be over 3× naive ones (DROP 1.34 vs 0.44), and that paired differences and K reader samples per question shrink the minimum detectable effect ([Miller](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.00640)) [V]. CLT intervals badly underestimate uncertainty below a few hundred datapoints ([Bowyer et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.01747)) [V], and LLMs are non-deterministic even at temperature 0 with fixed seeds ([Blackwell et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.03492)) [V]. No published paper estimates compile-to-compile variance for LLM-compiled knowledge artifacts. The project notes that compile noise is at least as large as the effects being measured, so reporting it would itself be new. As worked arithmetic from the notes (not from a source): with a 30% discordant rate, a paired comparison on 400 questions detects about **7.7 points** at 80% power, and 1,000 questions about 4.9. Question count cannot shrink the compile-variance term, which only falls as 1/C with C compiles per arm.

## Nine changes to WiCER's next experiments

The literature turns into a short, ordered list. The table puts loop changes first, measurement changes second and positioning third. The earlier compression review's recommendations still stand alongside these: test whether q8_0-K / q4_0-V KV quantization lets the full source fit, run equal-budget random-sentence and LLMLingua-2 baselines, and test omission markers for the unanswerable slice.

| # | Change | What it tests or fixes | Literature basis | Cost |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Guard-set acceptance rule**: keep a repair only if it improves probes *and* does not regress a frozen guard probe set drawn from unpinned documents | Held-out displacement (pinned +0.10 / unpinned −0.13, internal) | [WikiLoop](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.26604), [DeepRefine](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.10488), [SEAGym](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.17546) | Low: one more probe pass per iteration |
| 2 | **Delta edits instead of recompilation**: insert pinned facts as itemized edits into the existing wiki | Context collapse, and fresh compile noise each iteration | [ACE](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.04618), [BooookScore](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.00785) | Low; may also cut Haiku cost |
| 3 | **Coverage-controlled probes**: seed probes per document and fact type, filter for answerability, measure the fraction of RepLiQA gold paragraphs the probes hit | Probe→held-out gap caused by undersampling | [Pan & Yu](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.21829), [Cartridges](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.06266), [EntiGraph](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.07431), [Doc2Query--](https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.03266), [Sleep-time](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.13171) | Medium: more probe calls |
| 4 | **Report held-out by probe overlap**: buckets for same paragraph, same document and unrelated document | Turns the gap into a curve comparable with Pan & Yu | [Pan & Yu](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.21829) | Free with RepLiQA gold paragraphs |
| 5 | **Verbatim leaves under the wiki**: compile a short navigational summary and render detail-bearing sentences verbatim, or give the summary only to documents with low fact density | Paraphrase-driven detail loss | [LongMemEval](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.10813), [HippoRAG 2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.14802), [FlexA-Mem](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.33274), [KET-RAG](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.09304), [ReadAgent](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.09727) | Medium; extends the TACT render |
| 6 | **Question-free keyfact recall**: extract atomic facts per document once and label each as verbatim / paraphrased / altered / absent in the wiki, by fact type (number, date, name, other) | Measures omission and drift with hundreds of items per topic; lower noise than ~20 QA items | [FineSurE](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.00908), [MiniCheck](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.10774), [NumPrec/NEPrec](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.01248), [Signal and Noise](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.13144) | Medium once, then cached; validate on ~200 hand-labelled facts |
| 7 | **Two-stage error decomposition**: for each held-out question, is the gold fact in the wiki, and if so did the reader answer? Report P(present) × P(correct \| present) | Separates compile noise from reader failure | [VeriTrail](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.21786), [Sufficient Context](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.06037) | Low |
| 8 | **Statistics and judging**: ≥3–5 compiles per arm with shuffled document order, K≥3 reader samples, paired differences, topic→compile→question bootstrap, concrete-criterion judging from a non-Claude family | Compile noise ≥ effects; judge confounds | [Miller](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.00640), [Bowyer](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.01747), [Cochran](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.18490), [MSumBench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.00549), [Streaming KC](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.09877) | High in rental hours; the dominant budget item |
| 9 | **Unanswerables scored separately**, plus one external corpus | The ~20% unanswerable slice; external validity | [Han et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.11371), [LaRA](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.09977), [NoCha](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.16264), [Loong](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.17419), [SummHay](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.01370) | Free for the split; medium for the external corpus |

Items 1, 2 and 4 are cheap and attack the held-out gap directly, so they belong in the next run. Item 6 changes what counts as the primary outcome. Keyfact recall gives each topic hundreds of scored items instead of a few dozen questions, and that is the only realistic way to resolve effects smaller than compile noise without multiplying compile runs. Han et al.'s null-question collapse for community summaries (96 → 19) and LaRA's finding that small models lose most on hallucination detection under long context ([LaRA](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.09977)) [V] together predict that WiCER's unanswerable accuracy degrades as the wiki becomes more abstract. The project already observed this in TACT, and it should be reported as its own row rather than averaged in. For the external corpus, NoCha's paired true/false claims cancel guessing, and Loong requires every document for an answer ([NoCha](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.16264), [Loong](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.17419)) [V]. Avoid Wikipedia-based sets such as FRAMES, where parametric leakage confounds whether the wiki carried the answer ([FRAMES](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.12941)) [V]. One mechanism is worth testing because it is free: NoLiMa shows readers rely on literal overlap ([NoLiMa](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.05167)) [V]. Stratifying held-out accuracy by question–wiki lexical overlap would show whether paraphrase is what hurts.

What WiCER can defensibly claim as new, given this map, is fourfold: (1) the only evaluation of a compiled wiki read in full by a small model from KV cache; (2) omission rather than commission as the measured failure; (3) the first compile-to-compile variance estimates for LLM-compiled knowledge artifacts, once item 8 runs; and (4) a probe→held-out curve for an in-context store, comparable to Pan & Yu's agentic one.

## Conclusion

The literature changes how WiCER's two headline problems should be read. The compilation gap is not a Haiku artifact. It is the same detail loss that GraphRAG critics, memory benchmarks and long-summary studies all measured, and every field that fixed it did so by keeping verbatim text reachable. The probe vs held-out gap is not a WiCER bug either. Pan & Yu reproduced it in an agentic store, and the field's only working mitigations are breadth of question coverage and explicit guards against regression. WiCER's refine loop is currently an unguarded, full-rewrite version of what WikiLoop and ACE learned to do with guard sets and deltas. Those are the cheapest changes with the most literature behind them.

Strategically, WiCER's niche is narrow but real and currently uncontested: a small reader holding a whole compiled wiki in context, evaluated on omission. Its credibility will rest on measurement more than method. The neighbouring 2026 papers have been undone by judge disagreement and scorer bugs, and none reports compile-to-compile variance. A WiCER that reports keyfact recall with a hand-validated sample, held-out accuracy by probe overlap, and confidence intervals across several compile seeds would provide the first trustworthy evaluation in the LLM-wiki cluster. That contribution could outlast any single score.
