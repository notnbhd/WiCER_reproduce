# Reference Wiki: Arts, Culture, and Community Festivals (Source Collection, 2023–2024)

## 1. Virtual Reality Art

### 1.1 The Digital Canvas: A Voyage into VR Art [ackhsdsy]
- Author describes first VR art experience; argues VR shifts audience from observer to participant and lets artists create whole environments.
- **VirtuArt showcase**, evening of **September 8, 2023**, curated by VR artists **Emily O'Neil** and **Jacob Torres**. Black room, attendees in headsets.
- O'Neil's piece: a platform suspended in an abstract cosmos; physics bent, colors and shapes morphed in response to the visitor's movements; a "choir" of virtual elements reacted.
- Area of deep blue hues with sorrowful strings pulsing, described as an "ocean of grief."
- **October 15, 2023**: interview with **Jacob Torres**, whose exhibition **"Synthesis of Awe"** the author attended a few weeks later. Quote: "Imagine not just seeing an artist's emotion but standing in it."
- Interactivity: a shimmering light, when touched, triggered "a symphony of illumination and sound."
- **November 20, 2023**: piece by emergent VR artist **Sophia Langley**; warm sweet scent matched sunset visuals; virtual grass felt underfoot though user stood on a flat floor. Langley: memories created in VR "are as potent as any other."
- Accessibility argument: VR gives anyone with headset access to worlds by top artists, bypassing museum privilege.
- Collaboration noted between artists from game design and psychology.

  5. Technology: **Sarah Kim**, Dover Theater Collective; LED lighting and secondhand projectors; last production **January 2024**.
  6. Paint and lighting: **Bill Thomson**, painter; late-January production used silhouettes and lighting only.
  7. Construction illusions with fabric and foam: **Sandy Martinez** led a workshop **February 18, 2024**.
  8. Resourceful team: **Steve Johnson**, veteran director; Madison Avenue Theater's March 2024 production used fake flowers and DIY carpentry.
  9. Community involvement: **Amanda Lee**, production manager, Lakeside Theatre since **April 5, 2024**; donations from garage sales.

### 5.2 Top 10 Must-Try Traditional Dishes [bymllyqb]
1. **Paella Valenciana** (Valencia, Spain): saffron rice; first crafted mid-1800s; cooked over open flame.
2. **Tagine** (Morocco): staple since late 9th century; named for earthenware pot; influences from Berbers, Arabs, Andalusians.
3. **Nasi Lemak** (Malaysia): on **October 10, 2023**, Malaysia Culinary Heritage Association declared it the national dish; banana leaf; coconut rice, sambal, anchovies, peanuts, egg.
4. **Gumbo** (Louisiana): since early 18th century; West African, Native American, French, Spanish influences; okra, filé powder, andouille.
5. **Feijoada** (Brazil): journalist **Carlos Mendes**'s article "Feijoada: A Taste of Freedom," **September 7, 2024**; dates to the 1500s; black beans with pork and beef.
6. **Sushi** (Japan): origins to 4th century BC; Narezushi; modern nigiri in the **Edo period (1603–1868)**.
7. **Rogan Josh** (Kashmir): from Persian influence around the 15th century; Kashmiri chillies.
8. **Moussaka** (Greece): roots in the Ottoman Empire; eggplant, minced meat, béchamel.
9. **Peking Duck** (China): first documented in the Yuan Dynasty; historian **Wang Zhi**'s 2023 dissertation.
10. **Borscht** (Ukraine): originates in 14th-century traditions; beetroot, cabbage, potatoes.
