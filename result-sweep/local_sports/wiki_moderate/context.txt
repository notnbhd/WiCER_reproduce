## 1. Sleep and Athletic Performance

### 1.1 The Vital Role of Slumber (feature)
- Dr. Julianne Hart, sleep researcher, interviewed Sept 15, 2023. Sleep is "essential for cognitive function, physical health, and overall well-being."
- REM sleep is where much mental consolidation occurs. Sleep is divided into REM and Non-REM stages.
- Tyler Reynolds, semi-professional soccer player: tossed and turned before the 2023 season opener on Sept 2 due to nerves. Reported less energy, slower reaction time, and reduced mental sharpness. He then treated sleep schedules as seriously as training.
- Physiotherapist Alicia Montoya, interviewed Sept 22: human growth hormone, vital for tissue repair and muscle growth, is released during deep sleep. Poor sleep can cause decreased motivation, increased perceived exertion, and higher injury risk.
- General adult advice is 7–9 hours. Dr. Michael Thompson (sleep expert, Sept 29): athletes may need 9–10 hours depending on training intensity and physiology.
- Nutritionist Sarah Lin (Oct 5): magnesium (green leafy vegetables, nuts) supports sleep; avoid heavy meals near bedtime.
- Tech guru Jason Patel (Oct 12) demonstrated sleep trackers capturing duration, quality and stages.
- Dr. Hart's routine advice: consistent bed and wake times including weekends; reduce screen use before bed to limit blue light exposure.
- Physiotherapist Montoya recommends meditation or deep-breathing to calm the nervous system.
- Coach Daniel Becker (interviewed Oct 18) synchronises sleep habits across the whole team before big games.
- High school coach Christine Yang (Oct 25): teenagers need more sleep; schools and training compete for time.
- Sports psychologist Dr. Eric Newman (Oct 28): stress and travel across time zones disrupt sleep; he uses cognitive-behavioral strategies, mindfulness and sleep scheduling before and during travel.

### 1.2 Understanding the Impact of Sleep on Sports Performance and Recovery (feature)
- Dr. Lillian Brooks presented at the Annual Sports Science Symposium, New York City, Oct 12, 2023. Consistent 7–9 hours of sleep can significantly improve speed, accuracy and reaction time.
- Dr. Eric Sullivan, sports science consultant, began a collaboration with the New Haven Falcons (basketball) on Sept 5, 2023 to study and improve player sleep. Players' sleep was monitored with wearables tracking duration and quality.
- Rochester Royals (top-tier soccer club) announced on Sept 18, 2023 a partnership with a technology firm for a sleep-optimisation environment: sleep-friendly lighting in player residences, temperature-controlled mattresses, blackout curtains.
- Head of Performance Jenna Watkins: players report feeling more rested; training performance metrics rising.
- Phoenix Trackers (elite running group) introduced sleep kits from Sept 28, 2023: earplugs, eye masks, portable white-noise machines. Sleep consultants teach relaxation techniques.
- Study published Nov 3, 2023 by sports physician Dr. Maya Patel with Stanford University: athletes given sleep guidance recovered up to 30% faster from injury.
- Boulder Climbers (professional cycling team) integrated sleep monitoring devices on Sept 15, 2023, tracking sleep stages, heart rate and movement. Physiologist Dr. Owen Grant individualised training loads based on sleep data.
- Chelsea Bar, sports nutrition specialist, worked with the Austin Arrows (football team) from Oct 20, 2023 on pre-bedtime snacks with cherries, milk and almonds. The team regulates caffeine in hours before bed.

### 1.3 Sleep Summary Notes
- Sleep experts consistently cite 7–9 hours for adults; athletes may need 9–10.
- Tools cited: wearables, sleep kits, sleep-friendly bedroom environments, nutrition timing.
