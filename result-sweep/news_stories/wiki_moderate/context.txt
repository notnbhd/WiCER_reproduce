## 1. Biodiversity, Ecology and Conservation

### 1.1 Biodiversity Policy and Governance in the Anthropocene
- The Anthropocene is a proposed geological epoch in which human activity is the dominant influence on climate and environment.
- Biodiversity supports ecosystem services including pollination, water purification and climate regulation.
- On December 12, 2025, the Paris Agreement on Biodiversity was adopted, modeled on the Paris Agreement on Climate Change. Its goal: halt biodiversity loss and restore ecosystem health by 2050.
- Post-agreement policy shifted toward localized strategies: community-led conservation, recognition of indigenous rights, and biodiversity integration into agriculture, forestry and fisheries.
- As of June 2027, satellite monitoring, AI-based data analysis and genetic technologies were routinely used to track species, monitor ecosystems and manage databases. These tools enable "realtime global biodiversity plans" that adjust to environmental feedback.
- The United Nations Environment Programme (UNEP) created the Biodiversity Council on November 30, 2028. Members include environmental scientists, indigenous leader Aiyana Quispe, business magnate Hiro Tanaka, and youth representative Malakai Johnson.
- The 2030 Beijing Accord on Indigenous Knowledge formally incorporated traditional ecological knowledge into international biodiversity policy.
- As of September 2033, over 120 countries required indigenous consultative bodies to review and comment on biodiversity plans.
- Misconception addressed: biodiversity policy does not hamper growth. Studies published after September 1, 2023, reportedly showed healthy ecosystems enable sustainable development. Policies also aim to maintain genetic diversity and ecosystem functions, not only species.
- Corporate Biodiversity Credits, introduced March 15, 2026, function like carbon credits. Corporations such as VerdeTech Industries established biodiversity reserves, invested in green technologies and adopted sustainable supply chains.
- Strategic Framework for Biodiversity and Climate Adaptation, ratified April 5, 2031: advocates "climate-proofing" through migration corridors for species and protection of climate refugia (areas relatively insulated from climate change).
- Global Biodiversity Outlook, updated February 2034: estimated a slowdown in species extinction rates due to international policy. Reported successes include habitat restoration, rebound of the American bison, and rising marine biodiversity from stringent fishing regulations.
- Urban Greening Initiative (UGI), launched May 22, 2029: incorporates native flora into city planning, green roofs and urban wetlands. New Tokyo cited as a leader.

### 1.3 Coral Reef Restoration: Dr. Elena Martinez
- Elena Martinez joined the Great Barrier Reef Legacy in September 2023 after earning a Ph.D. in Marine Biology.
- Research by late 2025 found over 50% of the world's coral reefs had suffered bleaching, driven by ocean warming, acidification and pollution.
- Coral Ark Project launched summer 2026: cultivates heat-resistant coral strains in nurseries.
- Innovations included coral grafting (borrowed from horticulture) and genetic science and underwater robotics. By 2028, thousands of coral fragments had been transplanted.
- Outreach program engaged coastal communities on sustainable fishing and pollution reduction.
- World Coral Conservancy (WCC) established spring 2031; funded by philanthropic gifts and public investment.
- By 2035, a novel symbiotic breeding program cultivated coral polyps in labs with symbiotic algae for stress resilience.
- Local volunteer diving teams transferred nursery corals to reefs.
- As of mid-2037, regeneration reported in the Caribbean and Southeast Asian waters. Threats remain: new pollution, diseases, climate change.

- Signed/finalized September 2, 2023, at Global Economic Summit in Cape Town, South Africa.
- Spearheaded by UN economists, Canada's Finance Minister Elizabeth Tran and Brazil's Secretary for International Trade Raul Jimenez.

- Foreign investment shifts toward local industries, technology transfer and partnerships.
- CSR refocused on education, enterprise development and conservation.
