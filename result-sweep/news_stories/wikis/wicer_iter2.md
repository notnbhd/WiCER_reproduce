## 1. Priority Reference Facts

### 1.1 Biodiversity Governance and Ethics

- Paris Agreement on Biodiversity: the 2050 goal is to halt biodiversity loss and restore ecosystem health. Policies after it shifted toward community-led conservation, indigenous rights, and integrating biodiversity into agriculture, forestry, and fisheries.
- Ethics: no passage frames ethical principles as shaping biodiversity policy. Closest material:
  - The 2030 Beijing Accord on Indigenous Knowledge formally incorporated traditional ecological wisdom into international biodiversity policy.
  - As of September 2033, over 120 countries require indigenous consultative bodies to review and comment on biodiversity plans.
  - Studies published after September 1, 2023 showed healthy ecosystems enable sustainable development, countering the claim that biodiversity policy hampers growth.
  - Policies aim to maintain genetic diversity and ecosystem functions that give resilience to environmental change, not only species preservation.
  - Corporate Biodiversity Credits, introduced March 15, 2026, function like carbon credits.
- Policy-shaping factors in this material: stakeholder interests, the Biodiversity Council (November 30, 2028), and the misconception section. None are framed as ethics.
- The source does not name a "Paris Agreement on Biodiversity," "Resource Redistribution Treaty," or "Strategic Framework for Biodiversity and Climate Adaptation" in the summit/youth article (olwfdrxz).
- The youth summit article (olwfdrxz) contains no 2050 restoration target, no effects statement, and no discussion of agriculture, forestry, fisheries, indigenous rights, migration corridors, or climate refugia.
