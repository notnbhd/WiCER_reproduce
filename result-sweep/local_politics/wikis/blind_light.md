## 1. Fiscal Transparency and Public Access

### 1.1 Public Access to Fiscal Information (adwvfykb)
- Retrospective on local fiscal disclosure, beginning from the post-September 1, 2023 period.
- Early practice: annual budget summaries in local papers and town halls with dense figures.
- Judith Miller, former Little Rock council member, pushed in November 2023 for the first digitized budget uploaded to the municipal website.
- George Thompson, Sunshine Accountability Coalition, pushed for educational reforms.
- Spokane, Washington, by end of 2024: user-friendly budget visualizations and quarterly budget forums led by Sarah Jensen, then city finance director.
- Procurement: City of Austin set precedent in October 2025. Miguel Sanchez, city procurement officer, made contracts, bids, and awards public record with selection criteria explanations.
- Early 2026: corruption scandals in New Orleans administration exposed weak oversight.
- Data breaches and mismanagement illustrated the access-versus-security balance.
- El Paso: civil servant Maria Gonzalez launched the "Fiscal Literacy for All" campaign in February 2027, inviting public input into budgeting.
- May 2028: Detroit Accounting Software meltdown during reporting automation attempts.

### 1.2 Open Data Initiatives (afewuoyn)
- Open data: freely available government data in structured, usable form; growing since early 2010s.
- **Springfield**: Mayor Luisa Martinez launched the open data portal "Springfield Insights" on October 15, 2023. Contents: city budget details, public transportation statistics, infrastructure projects, public meeting minutes. Features: interactive charts, robust search.
- Local startups built apps from the portal for pothole reporting and real-time transit schedules.
- December 6, 2023 council meeting: Martinez said the launch "signifies our commitment to an open government."
- **Rivertown**: Mayor Elijah Warner launched "Rivertown Records" on January 9, 2024, including a public discourse forum.
- Rivertown saw a 20% increase in town hall attendance and a notable rise in citizen-led initiatives. Warner press release, February 28, 2024: "a bridge between City Hall and the people we serve."
- **Eastville**: "Eastville Open" launched November 1, 2023. Problems: data standardization, understaffed IT department, outdated and incomplete data.
- January 15, 2024 Eastville council meeting: citizens worried about misuse of sensitive information.
- Best practices: strong leadership, funding, strategic planning, data inventory, privacy screening, accuracy and timeliness, citizen workshops, data literacy tutorials.
- Martinez State of the City address, March 5, 2024: "Open data is the future of transparent governance."
