# Wiki: Arts, Culture and Community Reference (Sept 2023 to 2033)

## 1. Performance Art

### 1.1 The New Wave of Performance Art (hmgsvxnd)
- Alec Ramirez, a 24-year-old performer, transformed an abandoned warehouse into an immersive "theater" on October 15 (2023), blurring performer and audience boundaries. His piece "Converse in Verse" engaged passersby as spectators and contributors.
- Maria Zhang's "Rooftop Rhythms," initiated November 29 (2023), staged performances on city rooftops: interpretive dance and spoken word against the skyline.
- James "Lightfoot" Harris and his crew mixed breakdance, krumping and martial arts. Their piece "Battlefield Within" premiered December 3 (2023).
- Chloe Park's "Pavement Patterns," debuting September 21 (2023), uses unannounced flash-mob-style performances with a dancer troupe on sidewalks, on themes of connectivity and urban isolation.
- DJ Vex performs remixes of everyday sounds with electronic beats; his impromptu gigs included October 5 (2023) at City Park.
- Emma Liu's "Silent Crescendo" (premiered November 12, 2023) juxtaposed silence with bursts of sound, on digital noise versus human need for quiet.
- Leo Torres introduced "Dialogues in Motion" on September 10 (2023): viewers move alongside performers in choreographed, spontaneous dialogue.
- Katie Simmons's "Sensory Threads" (launched December 16, 2023): audience touch, sound and scent alter the outcome.
- Julian Fletcher's "Virtually Yours" (unrolled September 30, 2023) was a VR experience whose story changed with participants' decisions.
- Zara Patel's "Insta-Moments" (since September 17, 2023) uses Instagram to create a live documentary of street performances, including audience reactions.
- Benjamin Brooks's "Urban Echoes" (November 22, 2023) explored homelessness through performance.
- Luna Martinez's "Chroma Chronicles" (December 13, 2023) told multicultural identity stories through costume, music and movement.

### 1.2 Performance Art and Digital Tech (jslrqdze)
- Jameson DuPree's "Sensory Overload" series in New York City since September 7, 2023, uses augmented reality (AR) headsets for spectator participation. His piece "Fractured Horizons" has viewers construct and deconstruct the space around a live dance narrative.
- Code Motion (Los Angeles), first show October 15, 2023, uses AI to generate choreography from dancers in motion-capture suits. Founder Alex Rivera.
- Clara Kim's "Resonant Tides" (November 21, 2023, San Francisco Sound Art Festival): performers move through a sensor-rigged space, triggering soundscapes; uses binaural beats and spatial sound.
- Luca Garcia's "Ephemeral Canvas" (December 3, 2023, Atlanta, roundabout theatre): live painting animated by projection mapping.
- Evelyn Torres's VR piece "Boundless Realms" (launched January 8, 2024, Seattle).
- Hiro Tanaka's "CodeStream" (first performed February 20, 2024, Denver): live coding producing generative art in real time.
- Sophia Cortez (Miami) "The Collective Pulse," livestreamed March 14, 2024: audience feeds integrated into visuals.

### 1.3 Shock Revolution (yxybhwyo)
- Dakota Jones launched "Synapses Alight" on September 3, 2023, at a small gallery.
- Alejandra Herrera's "Echoes of the Unseen" opened October 21 (2023): sensory labyrinth on anxieties.
- Marion Dupont's guerrilla "freeze mobs" (September 15, 2023, city square): 50 individuals stopped moving for five minutes.
- Vincent Zhao's "Rebirth through Fire" (November 11, 2023, neighborhood park): artist emerged from flames.
- Luna García's "Quantum Lives" (December 8, 2023): scripted audience roles, quantum-leaping through historical moments.
- "Breathing Spaces" (January 12, 2024): public transit hubs as storytelling stages with commuters as narrators.
- Eliza Kwon's "Flesh and Binary," February 20 (2024): dance, light and live coding on identity and technology.
- Jayden Clarke's "Vestiges of the Disavowed" engages touch, sound and smell.

## 2. Visual Art, VR and Digital Media

### 2.1 The Digital Canvas: VR Art (ackhsdsy)
- "VirtuArt" showcase, September 8, 2023, curated by VR artists Emily O'Neil and Jacob Torres. Visitors entered an abstract cosmos by O'Neil; shapes responded to movement.
- Interview with Jacob Torres on October 15, 2023 (his VR exhibition "Synthesis of Awe," attended weeks later): "Imagine not just seeing an artist's emotion but standing in it."
- An "ocean of grief" section in deep blue with sorrowful strings.
- Sophia Langley, emerging VR artist, piece on November 20, 2023: scent of sunsets, virtual grass underfoot. She said memories made in VR are "as potent as any other."
- Argument: VR democratizes art; "art without borders."

### 2.2 Immersive VR Filmmaking (dznaltfk)
- Emily Jacobsen, cinematographer, introduced the Oculix 3000 camera in December 2023: 360-degree, dynamic range rivaling traditional film, real-time stitching.
- Screenwriter Roberto García penned "Whispers of the Ancients" (released February 10, 2024, award-winning VR experience).
- Actress Anna Liu (interview October 5, 2023) on the VR "Midsummer Night's Dream": "theater in the round on steroids."
- Editor Michael Zhang, "Orbit's Odyssey" (launched September 17, 2023), guided viewers via spatial audio and visual cues.
- Documentary "Through Their Eyes" (released November 1, 2023) placed viewers in refugees' lives.
- Sundance New Frontier expanded its VR section by January 2024.

9. Peking Duck (China): Yuan Dynasty; historian Wang Zhi, 2023 dissertation.
