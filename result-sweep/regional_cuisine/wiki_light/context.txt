## 2. Andalusian Cuisine and Desserts

### 2.1 Historical Roots of Andalusian Cuisine (akmnkbky)
- Region: Andalusia, southern Spain.
- Arabic influence lasted nearly eight centuries, starting with the Moorish invasion in 711.
- 2023: historian Dr. Fernanda Gómez discovered an ancient Arab cookbook.
- Staple spices introduced: cumin, saffron, cinnamon. Almonds and honey shaped pastries.
- Dishes with records dating to the 12th century: 'Pollo al Ajillo' (garlic chicken) and 'Jamoncillo de Piñones' (pine nut sweets).
- 1492: Columbus's voyage originated from the port of Palos de la Frontera.
- Columbian Exchange brought tomatoes, potatoes, peppers, beans. Gazpacho (originally stale bread, water, vinegar, garlic) was transformed by tomatoes and bell peppers.
- Chef José Rodríguez of Seville's restaurant "Raíces," October 15, 2023: innovation while respecting recipes keeps cuisine vibrant.
- Jewish heritage (before 1492 expulsion): 'adafina,' a slow-cooked Sabbath stew of meat, chickpeas, spices. Local chef Elena Suárez recreated a 14th-century recipe December 8, 2023.
- 'Pan de Cádiz': marzipan confection with candied fruit, linked to Jewish pastry traditions.
- Reconquista (1492 onward): return of pork. 'Jamón Ibérico,' 'Chorizo,' 'Cochinillo asado' (roast suckling pig) became Christian-era symbols.
- Historian Marta Vargas lecture on pork as cultural marker, September 4, 2023.
- 16th–18th centuries: convent and monastery sweets. 'Yemas de San Leandro' (egg yolk sweets) and 'Tocino de cielo' (egg yolk and sugar syrup dessert) used surplus yolks left from wine clarification with egg whites.
- 19th century agricultural boom: 'Rabo de Toro' (oxtail stew), 'Perdiz estofada' (partridge stew). Duke of Medinaceli's estate hosted banquets.
- Chef Carlos García, Prix d'Or-winning, opened "Sabor Ancestral" January 5, 2024. Reinvented 'Pescaíto Frito' (fried fish, a staple since Phoenician times).

### 2.2 Historical Roots of Andalusian Cuisine (oeubdhba)
- Phoenicians (11th century BC) introduced olives and wine; Romans continued agricultural practices.
- 8th century AD: Moors introduced irrigation, rice, citrus fruits, cumin, saffron.
- Gazpacho lineage: Romans made bread, olive oil, garlic pounded in mortar; Moors added vinegar; New World tomatoes and peppers after 1492.
- Tapas legend: Alfonso X, "The Wise King" (13th century), ill health limited him to small amounts of food with wine between meals.
- Golden Age (16th–17th centuries): New World foods (potatoes, tomatoes, bell peppers, chocolate). Aristocratic tables in Seville and Cádiz.
- Anonymously authored "Libro de cozina" (late 16th century) includes rabo de toro and chocos (cuttlefish).
- Rural staples: migas (fried breadcrumbs with chorizo), puchero (meat and vegetable stew).
- Spanish Civil War and dictatorship brought rationing. Gastronomic renaissance after return to democracy in 1975.
- Chef Dani García: modern reinterpretations of traditional dishes.
- November 17, 2023: Málaga hosted the third annual Andalusian Culinary Heritage Symposium.
- Present-day dishes: pescaíto frito (coastal towns), morcilla (blood sausage, mountains).

### 2.3 The Andalusian Dessert Palette: Sweet Endings from Southern Spain
- Moorish legacy: sugar, almonds, honey. Reconquest ended 1492.
- 'Pestiños': honey-glazed or sugar-dusted fritters, originally made in convents. Pastry chef Carmen Ruiz demonstrated at the Virgen de la Victoria festival in Málaga, September 8, 2023.
- Almonds in 'turrón' and 'bienmesabe'. Citrus zest in 'tortas de aceite.'
- Pastry historian Juan García, Seville masterclass October 27, 2023: eggs (symbol of new life) in 'tocino de cielo' ("bacon from heaven").
- Artisan María Lopez, Granada workshop December 12, 2023: handmade 'alfajores' filled with nuts, breadcrumbs, spices.
- 'Roscos de vino': doughnut-shaped, aniseed and wine. Confectioner Antonio Fernández (Jerez) explained, February 1, 2024.
- Chef Luisa Jiménez (Córdoba), April 5, 2024: deconstructed 'leche frita' (fried milk).
- Andalusian Culinary Institute courses since September 2023; director José Paredes.
- Small-scale producer Ana Gómez showcased organic almond flour at a local fair, September 15, 2024 (Málaga almond groves).
- Festival sweets: 'mantecados,' 'polvorones' at Feria de Abril (Seville). Others: 'bizcocho borracho' (tipsy cake), 'arroz con leche,' 'natillas.'
