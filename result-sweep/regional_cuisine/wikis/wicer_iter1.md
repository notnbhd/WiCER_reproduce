## 2. Andalusian Cuisine and Desserts

### 2.1 Historical Roots (Two Overviews) [akmnkbky, oeubdhba]
- Andalusia: southern autonomous community of Spain, Mediterranean coast.
- Phoenicians (11th century BC) introduced olives and wine; Romans followed with agriculture.
- Moorish invasion, 711; Arab rule nearly eight centuries. Introduced cumin, saffron, cinnamon, almonds, honey, rice, citrus, irrigation.
- The Arab-influence dishes: 'Pollo al Ajillo' (garlic chicken) and 'Jamoncillo de Pinones' (pine nut sweets), first mentioned in 12th-century records.
- 2023: historian Dr. Fernanda Gómez discovered an ancient Arab cookbook.
- Columbus sailed from Palos de la Frontera (1492). Columbian Exchange brought tomatoes, potatoes, peppers, beans.
- Gazpacho: originally stale bread, water, vinegar, garlic; tomatoes and bell peppers added later.
- Chef José Rodríguez of Seville's "Raíces" (October 15, 2023): innovation while respecting historic recipes.
- Jewish Andalusians before 1492 expulsion: 'adafina,' a slow-cooked Sabbath stew with meat, chickpeas, spices. Chef Elena Suárez recreated a 14th-century recipe on December 8, 2023.
- 'Pan de Cádiz': marzipan confection with candied fruit, linked to Jewish pastry traditions.
- Reconquista (1492 and after): reintroduction of pork. 'Jamon Iberico,' 'Chorizo,' 'Cochinillo asado' (roasted suckling pig).
- Historian Marta Vargas lecture September 4, 2023: pork as cultural marker.
- 16th–18th century convents: 'Yemas de San Leandro' (egg yolk sweets) and 'Tocino de cielo' (egg yolk and sugar syrup), made from surplus yolks left after wine clarification.
- 19th century landowner cuisine: 'Rabo de Toro' (oxtail stew), 'Perdiz estofada' (partridge stew). Duke of Medinaceli's estate hosted banquets.
- Golden Age (16th–17th c.): Seville and Cádiz aristocratic feasts; "Libro de cozina" late 16th century; chocos (cuttlefish).
- Rural dishes: migas (fried breadcrumbs with chorizo), puchero (meat and vegetable stew).
- Tapas legend: Alfonso X, 'The Wise King' (13th century), ill health, wine and small food between meals.
- Civil War and dictatorship: rationing. Return to democracy 1975 renaissance. Dani García reinterpretations.
- November 17, 2023: Málaga hosted the third annual Andalusian Culinary Heritage Symposium.
- Pescaíto frito (fried fish), morcilla (blood sausage).
- Gazpacho described as transformed through the Moors' vinegar addition.

**Preserved fact [kquyplig]:** Dr. Emily Frost, thermal engineer, led a workshop on October 3, 2023, on the science of heat transfer. She explained that in wood-fired ovens, heat is transferred in three ways: conduction, convection, and radiation. Conduction occurs when heat moves between objects in direct contact, such as the oven floor transferring heat to the bottom of a pizza. Convection circulates hot air within the oven, ensuring uniform temperatures and cooking rates. Radiation emits heat energy in the form of waves, evenly browning the surface of the food.
