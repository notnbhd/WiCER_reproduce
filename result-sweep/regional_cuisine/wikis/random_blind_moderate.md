## 1. Wok Cooking and Stir-Frying

### 1.1 Chef Liang Zhao and Flame's Embrace [adknmljy]
- Chef Liang Zhao runs the restaurant "Flame's Embrace," which opened September 15, 2023.
- Zhao says the wok originated in Guangdong province and has a history dating to the Han Dynasty.
- The wok's high sides and rounded bottom allow quick cooking and even heat distribution.
- Zhao's favorite wok was crafted October 3, 2023, by a master wok-maker in Yunnan.
- Seasoning (coating with oil and heating until smoking) was carried out by Zhao on October 11.
- SousChef Anna Lin demonstrated ingredient preparation on October 21.
- Zhao showed recipes reinterpreted from ancient originals on October 30.
- Zhao demonstrated wok heating over gas flames on November 2.
- The restaurant's grand opening gala was November 22.
- Zhao acquired his chuan (spatula) December 1.
- A new handcrafted ceramics line was unveiled December 15.
- Nutritionist Emily Wong, at a cooking demo on January 5, said quick wok cooking retains more nutrients and requires less oil.
- Consistency checks have been implemented since February 10.
- On March 1, Zhao presented a fusion dish combining Szechuan pepper with Argentinean chimichurri.
- Zhao gave a community cooking class March 18.
- Stir-fry order: proteins first, then vegetables, then aromatics; sauce and herbs added at finish.

### 1.2 Wok Mistakes and Fixes [mpxnkgvj]
- Gordon Lee revealed his "Wok Star" stir-fry on live television September 17, 2023. Quote: "To wield a wok, one must first master the flames."
- Wok hei: the smoky char from high heat. Key is control, not maximum heat.
- Mei Lin (a home cook, not the Top Chef contestant) burned vegetables in October 2023.
- Culinary blogger Jake "The Snake" Anderson described oil spills on November 3, 2023. Advice: use high-smoke-point oils, swirl to coat, do not pour pools.
- Culinary instructor Sarah Jenkins championed "Wok in Batches" from mid-September 2023: cook in small sequential batches to avoid steaming.
- Peter Chang's beef turned watery on September 29, 2023; advice: dry protein and bring to room temperature before searing.
- Tony "Too Much Sauce" Rodriguez's oversaucing was reported December 14, 2023.
- Emily Zhang (September 24, 2023) recommended mise en place: prep all ingredients before heating.
- Tim "Flip" Nicholson's flip on October 21 sent vegetables across the kitchen; advice: flip with control, keep food inside the wok.

### 1.3 Wok Cooking Summary
- Overall guidance: control heat, use correct oil quantity, cook in portioned batches, prep protein dry, apply sauce sparingly, have ingredients ready, flip safely.

## 2. Andalusian Cuisine

### 2.1 History and Arabic Influence [akmnkbky, oeubdhba]
- Andalusia is in southern Spain; the Moorish invasion began 711; Arab influence lasted nearly eight centuries.
- Introduced Arab staples: cumin, saffron, cinnamon, almonds, honey, rice, citrus, advanced irrigation.
- 2023: historian Dr. Fernanda Gómez discovered an ancient Arab cookbook.
- Dishes first mentioned in 12th-century records: Pollo al Ajillo (garlic chicken), Jamoncillo de Piñones (pine nut sweets).
- Phoenicians (11th century BC) introduced olives and wine; Romans followed with agriculture.
- Gazpacho: originally Roman bread, olive oil, garlic pounded in a mortar; Moors added vinegar; tomatoes and peppers from the New World after 1492 transformed it.
- Tapas legend: during the 13th-century reign of Alfonso X ("The Wise King"), illness supposedly led to small plates with wine; tapas later resembled modern versions after the reconquest.

### 2.2 Columbian Exchange [akmnkbky, oeubdhba]
- Columbus's 1492 voyage departed from the Andalusian port of Palos de la Frontera.
- Introduced tomatoes, potatoes, peppers, beans, chocolate.
- Chef José Rodríguez of Seville's "Raíces" spoke October 15, 2023, on innovation while respecting historical recipes.

### 2.3 Jewish Heritage [akmnkbky]
- Pre-1492 Jewish Andalusians made "adafina," a slow-cooked Sabbath stew with meat, chickpeas, spices.
- Chef Elena Suárez recreated a 14th-century adafina recipe December 8, 2023.
- "Pan de Cádiz": marzipan confection with candied fruit, linked to Jewish pastry traditions.

### 2.4 Reconquista and Pork [akmnkbky]
- Post-1492 reintroduction of pork. Jamón Ibérico, chorizo, cochinillo asado (roast suckling pig) became Christian-era symbols.
- Historian Marta Vargas lectured September 4, 2023, on pork as cultural marker.

### 2.5 Convents and Monasteries (16th–18th c.) [akmnkbky]
- Yemas de San Leandro (egg yolk sweets) and Tocino de cielo (yolk and sugar syrup dessert) originated in convents.
- Nuns used surplus yolks left from clarifying wine with egg whites.
- Convents still sell pastries.

### 2.6 Landowner Cuisine (19th–early 20th c.) [akmnkbky]
- Agricultural boom; dishes: Rabo de Toro (oxtail stew), Perdiz estofada (partridge stew).
- The Duke of Medinaceli's estate hosted grand banquets.

### 2.7 Modern Andalusian Developments [akmnkbky, oeubdhba]
- Prix d'Or-winning chef Carlos García opened "Sabor Ancestral" January 5, 2024; reinvented Pescaíto Frito (fried fish, Phoenician-era technique).
- Dani García: avant-garde chef reinterpreting traditional dishes.
- Post-1975 return to democracy spurred gastronomic renaissance.
- Málaga hosted the third annual Andalusian Culinary Heritage Symposium November 17, 2023.
- Spanish Civil War and dictatorship brought rationing.
- Dishes: migas (fried breadcrumbs with chorizo), puchero (meat and vegetable stew), morcilla, pescaíto frito.
- "Libro de cozina," an anonymous late-16th-century cookbook, mentions rabo de toro and chocos (cuttlefish).

### 2.8 Andalusian Dessert Palette [zxrzlgef]
- Moorish legacy: almonds, sugar, honey, citrus.
- Pestiños: honey-glazed fritters; Carmen Ruiz demonstrated at Virgen de la Victoria festival, Málaga, September 8, 2023.
- Turrón, bienmesabe (almond), tortas de aceite (crisp citrus treat).
