## Urban Development, Traffic and Transit

### Springfield Heights Traffic Gridlock (source: wrmyxyjk)
- 12 Sept 2023: a traffic snarl in Springfield Heights made the front page of every local news outlet. Commuter Sarah Johnson, who juggles two jobs and parenthood, said: "It took me two hours to drive what usually is a thirty-minute journey. It's exhausting and unsustainable."
- 20 Sept 2023, town hall: city planner Michael Thompson explained, "We are dealing with a multifaceted problem. It's not only about the number of cars on the roads but also about poor urban planning, limited public transport options, and lack of real-time traffic management." Springfield Heights has seen a surge in vehicle ownership without a corresponding expansion of road infrastructure.
- Bus fleet described as small, outdated and often off-schedule.
- Urban transport expert Dr. Linda Cartwright advocated road-sharing strategies prioritizing high-occupancy vehicles and public transit.
- 5 Oct 2023: city council proposed a high-occupancy vehicle (HOV) lane on the busiest avenue, for vehicles with three or more passengers, including buses.
- Carpool incentives include reduced parking fees. Local business owner Rajiv Menon supported carpooling for employee commutes.
- 15 Nov 2023 proposal: increase bus fleet by 40% over three years, incorporating electric models. Advocacy group leader Emily Torres called for more frequent, reliable buses.
- Real-time bus tracking app planned.
- Tech entrepreneur Jasmine Iyer, conference 30 Sept 2023: AI-driven traffic management is "not science fiction; it's a practical solution."
- "Commute Smart" initiative launched 25 Oct 2023: local volunteers conduct surveys and promote public transit.
- Policy options: congestion charges at peak hours, tax benefits for businesses adopting flexitime, parking and traffic-flow requirements for new buildings, public-private sponsorship of bike-share stations and transit passes.
