## Stockholm and Helsinki: Trends in Tech Startup Investments and Funding

Stockholm and Helsinki's landscapes have changed dramatically in recent years when it comes to the way tech startups are funded and invested. The differences in economic scales and cultural backgrounds affect the concentration and abundance of venture capital companies, the availability and success of crowdfunding. The cornerstones of startup financing are venture capital (VC), angel investors and crowdfunding platforms. These funding avenues in Stockholm and Helsinki have shown unique trends and patterns as we approach September 2023.
Many of Stockholm's successful tech startups have been driven by venture capital.
Stockholm's technology sector has seen an influx in international venture capital since the success of companies such as Spotify and Klarna.
Andreas Johansson declared in November 2023, that the "Stockholm tech ecosystem" offers venture capitalists an unique blend of advanced technologies, innovative business model, and skilled entrepreneurs ready for scalable growth. Helsinki has also started to attract the attention of European Venture Capitalists, thanks to its thriving tech hub.
Mediflow, a Helsinki digital health startup secured a EUR20 million investment round in early 2024 from a consortium.
Angel investors are crucial in the early stages.
There are now ventures like "Stockholm Angels", where seasoned entrepreneurs such as Lena Olving, Johan Stael von Holstein and others pool their resources in order to support startups.
The Helsinki Angels Network was founded by Jukka Pekka Makinen and has played a key role in the development of new tech companies.
Crowdfunding is a growing part of startup financing, allowing the public to directly support innovation.
Stockholm's entrepreneurs have welcomed crowdfunding, and platforms such as FundedByMe are now a fixture in the startup scene. In January 2024, EkoGadget a techhardware startup raised SEK 10,000,000 through a successful crowdfunding campaign.
Finnish startups use crowdfunding more as a supplement to other forms of funding than a primary source. Invesdor, for example, has facilitated several important funding rounds by combining crowdfunding with traditional methods of investment to create a hybrid approach tailored specifically to the Finnish market.
Sweden and Finland both have governments that are keen to foster innovation through legislation and initiatives. Each country has developed its own startup scene, and each has found their own niche. The Swedish government leveraged Stockholm’s international reputation through initiatives such as the “Stockholm Innovation Scholarship,” which rewards and finances promising local startups.
The Finnish government, on the other hand, offers substantial R&D incentives to support Helsinki's startup culture. The government has implemented "Business Finland" which offers funding and internationalization services to innovative startups.
Experts such as Sofia Lundberg a tech analyst predict that cross border investments will become more common as startups look to take advantage of the best features of both ecosystems.
