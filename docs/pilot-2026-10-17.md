# Pilot: `local_economy`, 2026-10-17

One-topic E3 run on rented hardware, to find out whether the pipeline
reproduces the paper before committing a 17-topic sweep. It does, with one
large qualification and one discovery about the corpus that invalidated the
first two attempts.

## Configuration

| | |
|---|---|
| Reader | Llama 3.1 8B Instruct Q5_K_M, `n_ctx=98304`, q8_0 KV, flash attention |
| Hardware | RTX 3090 24GB (rented), CUDA 12.4, `sm_86` |
| Compiler + judge | `anthropic/claude-haiku-5.5` via OpenRouter, `effort=high`, `max_tokens=64000` |
| Corpus | `repliqa_0`, 80 shortest documents/topic, 400 QA |
| Arms | E3 only: `fc_raw`, `wiki_{light,moderate,aggressive}`, WiCER loop, `random_pin` |
| Wall clock | 79 minutes (15:12-16:31); setup a further ~12 minutes |
| API cost | ~$1.20 |

## Headline: the paper's metric reproduces

Paper Table 4 evaluates WiCER on the probe set.

| `local_economy` | paper | ours |
|---|---|---|
| Blind (moderate) | 2.16 | 3.16 |
| Best WiCER | 3.24 | 3.66 |
| FC raw | 3.48 | 3.62 |
| **Recovery** | **82%** | **108%** |

FC raw also clears the spec's 3.46 target (3.62, +0.17). The ablation —
the load-bearing comparison, since it separates "diagnosis works" from
"pinning anything works" — reproduces directionally:

| | blind | random pin | WiCER |
|---|---|---|---|
| ours | 3.16 | 3.20 (+0.00) | 3.66 (**+0.50**) |
| paper | — | +0.16 | +0.95 |

## Qualification: the gain does not generalise

This repo scores held-out questions the loop never saw (`DEVIATIONS.md` 12),
an instrument the paper does not have. The two disagree sharply:

| question set | blind | WiCER | FC raw | recovery |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| probe set (paper's metric) | 3.16 | 3.66 | 3.62 | **108%** |
| held-out | 2.78 | 2.86 | 3.62 | **10%** |

A +0.50 gain on diagnosed questions becomes +0.08 on undiagnosed ones.
Pinned facts consume budget that would otherwise carry general coverage —
the displacement effect of the paper's Appendix I — and on this topic it
consumes nearly all of the apparent benefit.

**Any recovery rate quoted from this pipeline must say which question set it
came from.** The two differ by a factor of ten on the same run.

## Our blind baseline is a full point above the paper's

3.16 against 2.16. The likeliest cause is the compiler: Haiku 5.5 writes much
better blind wikis than the paper's 2025-era Sonnet, which compresses the room
WiCER has left to improve and inflates recovery against a stronger denominator.

This means **our 108% and the paper's 82% are not measuring the same thing**,
even though both are computed correctly. A reproduction that changes the
compiler changes the baseline the method is credited against.

## What the corpus turned out to be

The first two attempts never produced a recovery rate at all: at the paper's
96K window, **16 of 17 topics overflowed** and `fc_raw` was skipped. The paper
reports only two such topics.

Measurement (full detail in `DEVIATIONS.md` 2) eliminated every explanation but
one. Not a newer dataset — the HuggingFace `sha` is what we build from. Not a
different split — `repliqa_0..4` agree on document length within 2%. Not
selection bias in the old rule — the lexicographically first 80 average
*exactly* the population mean. Not prompt scaffolding — 1.2%. Not a QA-count
filter — every document carries exactly 5 QA pairs.

What remained: the paper's 80 documents are **the shortest ones**. Selecting
on length reproduces its reported sizes within 0.4%, the count of
full-context exclusions (15/17 fit), and their identity — Regional Cuisine and
Regional Folklore, exactly the pair its Section 5 footnote 2 names.

| topic | ours (shortest-80) | paper | delta |
|---|---|---|---|
| Company Policies | 54,779 | 54,987 | −0.4% |
| Regional Folklore | 97,322 | 97,148 | +0.2% |
| Regional Cuisine | 97,427 | 97,251 | +0.2% |

The prior note in `DEVIATIONS.md` claimed the lexicographic rule "appears to
land on the same documents the paper used", and its own evidence disproved it:
48,459 words against 54,987 tokens implies 1.135 tokens/word, where the real
tokeniser gives 1.29–1.32.

This is a reconstruction, not a fact the paper states. Landing 0.2–0.4% off the
shortest-possible floor means it was not literally "sort and take 80" either.

## Measurement notes

**Compression targets are not met, and not monotonic.** Targets 75/50/25%
produced 19.0/12.5/13.1% — aggressive compressed *less* than moderate. The
paper reports 35.4/12.2/8.2%, so only moderate agrees. Over-compression is a
finding the paper also reports; the inversion is ours and unexplained.

**`Kept verbatim` reads 0%.** Haiku paraphrases pinned facts rather than
copying them, so the column cannot distinguish a dropped fact from a reworded
one. Probe quality still improved, so the facts are arriving — just not as
substrings. Check distinctive tokens, not string equality.

**The second iteration made things worse** (3.66 → 3.30, −9.9%), stopping on
`insufficient_improvement`. Iteration 1 carried the entire effect, while
iteration 2 pinned more facts (17 vs 13, 1,462 vs 1,157 words) and scored worse
— displacement again, visible within a single run.

**`effort=high` held at scale.** Every compile returned `finish_reason='stop'`;
the truncation guard never fired. Reasoning and a ~15K-token wiki coexist
inside `max_tokens=64000` on a 91K-token source.

**Latency reproduces directionally**: TTFT 0.175s → 0.055s, 31 → 82 tok/s
between `fc_raw` and the compiled wikis.

## Cost, and a trap in reading it

~$1.20 for the topic, so roughly **$20 for a 17-topic sweep** — inside the
key's $50 limit.

`judge_usage.json` is **cumulative across the process, not per condition**.
`benchmark/evaluate.py` scores every condition in one run and writes the judge
client's running totals into each condition's file, so the six files report
386 → 711 → 1021 → 1383 → 1728 → 2014 calls. Summing them triples the real
figure. Take the largest, not the sum.

`DRY_RUN=1` is no help either: it prices every model from a hardcoded $3/$15
table with no override, and overestimated the Haiku fixture pilot by 30–40×.

## Operational notes

* `nvidia/cuda:12.4.1-devel-ubuntu22.04` is the right rental image — the
  llama.cpp CUDA build needs `nvcc`, which the PyTorch runtime images lack.
* **`nvcc` will appear missing over SSH.** Docker `ENV` is not inherited by
  `sshd` sessions, so `/usr/local/cuda/bin` is off `PATH` even in a `devel`
  image. The toolkit is there; the path is not.
* A 3090 24GB beats a V100 32GB at equal price: flash attention is certain on
  Ampere and unreliable on Volta, llama.cpp cannot quantise the V cache without
  it, and bandwidth is a wash (936 vs 900 GB/s). Build with `CUDA_ARCH=86`.
* VRAM: 12,484 MiB at 96K, 14,820 MiB at 128K — both comfortable in 24 GB.
* One topic takes ~80 minutes, not the ~2.9 h estimated. `fc_raw` is 400
  questions in 11 minutes at 1.67 s/q, because prefix KV reuse means the 91K
  context is prefilled roughly once.

## Open questions

1. **Is the shortest-80 reconstruction right?** It matches the paper on three
   independent axes, but the paper states no rule. Matching sizes is not the
   same as holding the same documents.
2. **Does the Haiku judge shift absolute levels?** `benchmark/judge_agreement.py`
   exists for exactly this and has not been run. Paper Section 5 puts the FC/RAG
   gap at 0.18 points, so a condition-dependent judge bias of 0.17 could erase
   or invent E2 by itself. Cheap to check — 100 questions.
3. **Does the probe/held-out divergence hold across topics?** `local_economy`
   is one topic, and the paper's own Table 4 is a 15-topic mean.
4. **Why is compression non-monotonic across levels?**
5. **Regional Cuisine and Regional Folklore still have no FC baseline** and so
   no recovery rate — as in the paper.
