# CAG vs RAG, and how corpora are represented when compiled for an LLM

Scope: annotated bibliography, 2024–2026 (most entries 2025–2026). Every arXiv ID, title and first author below was checked against the arXiv API (export.arxiv.org) on 2026-11-07. Venues come from the arXiv comment field or the publisher page; where I could not confirm a venue it says "arXiv" only. Our setup: a corpus compiled into a fixed-budget wiki, preloaded into a reader LLM (CAG-style), evaluated on RepLiQA. Typed claim/triple layout lost to plain truncation; the same selection rendered as verbatim source sentences won by +0.56 on a 1–5 scale.

## Q1. CAG and long-context (LC) vs RAG

### Takeaway
CAG (preloading the corpus into a KV cache) is a one-paper idea (Chan et al., WWW'25 short paper). Most follow-ups are KV-reuse systems work or compression add-ons, not evaluations. The LC-vs-RAG literature agrees on one thing: there is "no silver bullet". LC wins on Wikipedia-style and evidence-dense QA, and RAG wins on sparse evidence, refusals/hallucination and cost. Length alone degrades reading even when retrieval is perfect, which is why a compiled, smaller context can beat raw full-context.

### Cited Findings
**Anchor papers**
- **WiCER: Wiki-memory Compile, Evaluate, Refine Iterative Knowledge Compilation for LLM Wiki Systems.** Huerta, arXiv 2026 (2605.07068). Finding: on 17 RepLiQA domains (6,800 Qs), full-context KV-cache inference beats RAG on curated knowledge (4.38 vs 4.08/5, 7.3× faster TTFT) but drops below RAG at scale. Blind compilation fails (2.14–2.32 vs 3.46; 53–60% catastrophic failures), and 1–2 probe-guided refine rounds recover about 80% (3.24 vs 3.47). Relevance: the paper we reproduce and extend. Its "compilation gap" is exactly where representation choice matters. — [arXiv:2605.07068](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.07068)
- **Don't Do RAG: When Cache-Augmented Generation is All You Need for Knowledge Tasks.** Chan et al., WWW 2025 (short paper) (2412.15605). Finding: preloading all documents into a precomputed KV cache and truncating query tokens between questions matches or beats RAG when the knowledge base fits in context, with no retrieval errors. Relevance: defines our serving model. It assumes the raw corpus fits; we compile because it doesn't fit at budget. — [arXiv:2412.15605](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.15605)
- **Enhancing Cache-Augmented Generation (CAG) with Adaptive Contextual Compression for Scalable Knowledge Integration.** Agrawal et al., arXiv 2025 (2505.08261). Finding: proposes dynamically compressing preloaded context, plus a hybrid CAG-RAG that retrieves when the cache is insufficient. Reports gains on scalability and multi-hop (abstract-level claims; I did not check the numbers). Relevance: closest explicit "CAG + compression" follow-up, and a direct competitor framing to a compiled wiki. — [arXiv:2505.08261](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.08261)
- An arXiv full-text search for "cache-augmented generation" in abstracts (2026-11-07) returned only 4 hits: Chan et al., Agrawal et al., a KV-cache-translation paper (2607.28979), and an unrelated one. CAG as a named line of evaluation work is thin. — [arXiv API query](https://export.arxiv.org/api/query?search_query=abs:%22cache-augmented%20generation%22)

**Long context vs RAG comparisons**
- **Long Context vs. RAG for LLMs: An Evaluation and Revisits.** Li (Xinze) et al., arXiv Dec 2024 (2501.01880). Finding: after filtering out questions answerable without context, LC generally beats RAG, especially on Wikipedia QA. **Summarization-based retrieval (RAPTOR) performs comparably to LC, while chunk-based retrieval lags.** RAG wins on dialogue and general queries. Relevance: summaries can match LC on QA, which conflicts with our finding that verbatim beats abstracted text. The likely reason is that their summaries are retrieved alongside leaves, not substituted for them. — [arXiv:2501.01880](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.01880)
- **LaRA: Benchmarking Retrieval-Augmented Generation and Long-Context LLMs – No Silver Bullet for LC or RAG Routing.** Li (Kuan) et al., arXiv 2025 (2502.09977; listed as ICML 2025 elsewhere, not confirmed on the arXiv page). Finding: 2,326 cases, 11 LLMs. The best choice depends on model size, long-context ability, context length, task type and chunk properties. LC is better at reasoning and comparison; RAG is more robust for hallucination detection and refusals. Relevance: RepLiQA has about 20% unanswerable questions, and LaRA predicts full-context/CAG readers do worse on abstention, which matches what our TACT run showed ("unanswerable worse"). — [arXiv:2502.09977](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.09977); summary of LC/RAG task split via [search digest of LaRA](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2502.09977)
- **Retrieval Augmented Generation or Long-Context LLMs? A Comprehensive Study and Hybrid Approach (Self-Route).** Li (Zhuowan) et al., EMNLP 2024 Industry (2407.16833). Finding: LC beats RAG on average when well resourced, but RAG is far cheaper. Self-Route lets the model pick RAG or LC per query. Relevance: the baseline routing idea, and a cheap fallback for questions the wiki can't answer. — [arXiv:2407.16833](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.16833)
- **Long-Context LLMs Meet RAG: Overcoming Challenges for Long Inputs in RAG.** Jin et al., arXiv 2024 (2410.05983; ICLR 2025 per my recollection, not confirmed). Finding: more retrieved passages first help and then hurt, because "hard negatives" distract. Proposes retrieval reordering and RAG-specific tuning. Relevance: adding more text to a budgeted context is not monotone, so the budget should be tuned. — [arXiv:2410.05983](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.05983)
- **In Defense of RAG in the Era of Long-Context Language Models (OP-RAG).** Yu et al., arXiv 2024 (2409.01666). Finding: **keeping retrieved chunks in original document order** gives an inverted-U quality curve with a sweet spot that beats full LC using far fewer tokens. Relevance: supports rendering selected sentences in source order, as we do in the verbatim render. Order preservation is a cheap way to keep connective flow. — [arXiv:2409.01666](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.01666)
- **Context Length Alone Hurts LLM Performance Despite Perfect Retrieval.** Du et al., Findings of EMNLP 2025 (2510.05381). Finding: across 5 LLMs, performance drops 13.9–85% as input grows, even with whitespace padding or masked irrelevant tokens. Prompting the model to recite the evidence first recovers up to 4% on RULER. Relevance: gives a mechanism for WiCER's "attention dilution" and a reason a compiled wiki can beat raw full-context. — [arXiv:2510.05381](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.05381)
- **Beyond RAG vs. Long-Context: Learning Distraction-Aware Retrieval for Efficient Knowledge Grounding.** Shim et al., ICLR 2026 (2509.21865). Finding: learns which passages distract the reader, beyond relevance. Relevance: suggests selecting wiki content by "helps the reader" rather than relevance alone. — [arXiv:2509.21865](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.21865)
- **When Retrieval Succeeds and Fails: Rethinking Retrieval-Augmented Generation for LLMs.** Wang (Yongjie) et al., arXiv Oct 2025 (2510.09106). Finding (via search snippet): LC is better when evidence is spread across documents, and RAG is better for sparse evidence when retrieval is accurate. — [arXiv:2510.09106](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.09106)
- **Route Before Retrieve: Activating Latent Routing Abilities of LLMs for RAG vs. Long-Context Selection.** Chen (Yiwen) et al., arXiv 2026 (2605.10235). An LLM-side router for RAG vs LC. — [arXiv:2605.10235](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.10235)
- **NoLiMa: Long-Context Evaluation Beyond Literal Matching.** Modarressi et al., ICML 2025 (2502.05167). Finding: long-context accuracy collapses when the question and the needle share no literal tokens. Relevance: **verbatim sentences keep the lexical overlap that RepLiQA questions share with source text, and triples/paraphrases remove it.** This is a plausible mechanism for our +0.56. — [arXiv:2502.05167](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.05167)
- **Summary of a Haystack: A Challenge to Long-Context LLMs and RAG Systems.** Laban et al., arXiv 2024 (2407.01370). Finding: both LC and RAG systems score low on coverage and citation in synthetic haystack summarization. — [arXiv:2407.01370](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.01370)
- **RepLiQA: A Question-Answering Dataset for Benchmarking LLMs on Unseen Reference Content.** Monteiro et al., arXiv 2024 (2406.11811). The benchmark: synthetic, unseen documents, so parametric knowledge can't help and the representation of the context is everything. — [arXiv:2406.11811](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.11811)

**KV-reuse and "compiled cache" systems (CAG infrastructure)**
- **Cartridges: Lightweight and general-purpose long context representations via self-study.** Eyuboglu et al., arXiv 2025 (2506.06266). Finding: naive next-token training of a small KV cache on the corpus is not competitive with in-context learning (ICL). "Self-study" (synthetic conversations about the corpus plus context distillation) matches ICL with 38.6× less memory and 26.4× higher throughput, and stays composable. Relevance: a *learned* alternative to a *text* wiki at a fixed budget, trained on synthetic Q&A probes much like WiCER's. — [arXiv:2506.06266](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.06266)
- **TurboRAG** (Lu et al., 2410.07590), **CacheBlend** (Yao et al., 2405.16444), **Block-Attention for Efficient Prefilling** (Ma et al., ICLR 2025, 2409.15355) and **KVLink** (Yang et al., 2502.16002) precompute per-chunk KV caches and fuse them at query time. Relevance: they make "preload compiled chunks" cheap, but they are systems papers with no evidence on representation. — [2410.07590](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.07590), [2405.16444](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.16444), [2409.15355](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.15355), [2502.16002](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.16002)
- **KBLaM: Knowledge Base augmented Language Model.** Wang (Xi) et al., arXiv 2024 (2410.10450; ICLR 2025, not confirmed on the arXiv page). Encodes KB *triples* as key-value vectors injected via rectangular attention. Relevance: a triple-based preload that needs training. It is the parametric cousin of our typed layout. — [arXiv:2410.10450](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.10450)
- Anthropic's Contextual Retrieval post (2024-09-19) recommends putting the whole KB in the prompt if it is under about 200k tokens; prompt caching cuts latency by more than 2× and cost by up to 90%. — [Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/news/contextual-retrieval)

### Inferences
- In the literature the CAG-vs-RAG question is settled as "it depends". The open question is *what to put in the cache* when the corpus exceeds the budget. WiCER and Cartridges are the only works I found that attack that directly, and they do it in text and in KV space respectively.
- NoLiMa together with Du et al. gives a two-part explanation of our results. Verbatim text keeps lexical anchors to the question; shorter context reduces dilution. Triples lose the first.

### Gaps
- I found no paper evaluating CAG specifically on RepLiQA other than WiCER, and no independent replication of Chan et al.'s numbers.
- I did not verify Agrawal et al.'s experimental numbers. It is an arXiv-only paper, so treat it as weak evidence.
- The ICML 2025 venue for LaRA and the ICLR 2025 venues for Jin et al. and KBLaM are unconfirmed.

## Q2. Proposition / atomic-fact retrieval units, and evidence they lose context

### Takeaway
Propositions help retrieval *precision per token* when the budget is small (Dense X, Li et al. NAACL'25). But a 2026 reproduction finds proposition chunking is the worst segmentation for in-corpus retrieval (−15–27%) because tiny units lose topical context. The fixes are adding context back (molecular facts, contextualized embeddings, proposition→passage links), which is consistent with our result.

### Cited Findings
- **Dense X Retrieval: What Retrieval Granularity Should We Use?** Chen (Tong) et al., arXiv Dec 2023, EMNLP 2024 (2312.06648). Finding: indexing by propositions ("atomic, self-contained factoids") beats passage indexing for retrieval and improves downstream QA *under a fixed token budget*. Relevance: the main pro-proposition result. Note that its propositions are decontextualized natural-language sentences, not triples, and that gains were measured at retrieval and short budgets. — [arXiv:2312.06648](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.06648)
- **Context-Efficient Retrieval with Factual Decomposition.** Li (Yanhong) et al., NAACL 2025 (2503.19574). Finding: preprocessing the corpus into semi-structured atomic facts improves QA *when retrieved text is limited*. Relevance: the closest pro-atomic counterpoint to our result at a fixed budget. Worth checking whether their gain survives at our budget and on detail-level RepLiQA questions. — [arXiv:2503.19574](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.19574)
- **Beyond Chunk-Then-Embed: A Comprehensive Taxonomy and Evaluation of Document Chunking Strategies for Information Retrieval.** Zhou et al., arXiv Feb 2026 (2602.16974). Finding: a reproduction of DenseX, LumberChunker and late chunking. Simple structure-based chunking (paragraph, fixed-size) beats LLM-guided methods for in-corpus retrieval. Proposition segmentation is consistently worst, with 15–27% degradation vs paragraph methods, attributed to lost topical context. Contextualized embedding helps propositions most. Relevance: **the strongest independent evidence that propositions lose context**, matching "plain truncation beats typed layout". — [arXiv:2602.16974](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.16974); numbers via [search digest](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.16974v1)
- **Molecular Facts: Desiderata for Decontextualization in LLM Fact Verification.** Gunjal & Durrett, arXiv 2024 (2406.20079). Finding: fully atomic claims are ambiguous out of context. "Molecular facts" add the minimal context needed to stand alone (decontextuality vs minimality) and improve verification in ambiguous settings. Relevance: names our "dangling anaphora" failure and gives a design rule. Claims should carry the minimum disambiguating context, and verbatim sentences often already do. — [arXiv:2406.20079](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.20079)
- **Mix-of-Granularity: Optimize the Chunking Granularity for Retrieval-Augmented Generation.** Zhong et al., COLING 2025 (2406.00456). Finding: the best granularity varies per query, so a router picks the granularity. Relevance: argues against one fixed unit (claims) for all questions. — [arXiv:2406.00456](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.00456)
- **LongRAG: Enhancing Retrieval-Augmented Generation with Long-context LLMs.** Jiang et al., arXiv 2024 (2406.15319). Finding: using long retrieval units (about 4K tokens) with a long-context reader beats short units. Relevance: the opposite direction to Dense X; bigger units preserve context. — [arXiv:2406.15319](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.15319)
- **Is Semantic Chunking Worth the Computational Cost?** Qu et al., arXiv 2024 (2410.13070). Finding: semantic chunking's cost isn't justified by consistent gains over fixed-size chunking. — [arXiv:2410.13070](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.13070)
- **PropRAG: Guiding Retrieval with Beam Search over Proposition Paths.** Wang (Jingjin) et al., EMNLP 2025 (2504.18070). Propositions plus beam search over proposition paths for multi-hop. Relevance: propositions help when *linked*, in multi-hop settings unlike RepLiQA. — [arXiv:2504.18070](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.18070)
- **A Navigational Approach for Comprehensive RAG via Traversal over Proposition Graphs (ToPG).** Delmas et al., arXiv Jan 2026 (2601.04859). Finding: notes that KG-based RAG "suffers on fact-oriented single-hop queries". The fix is a heterogeneous graph of **propositions + entities + passages**. Relevance: even pro-proposition designs keep the passage. RepLiQA is mostly single-hop fact lookup, where KG-style formats are weakest. — [arXiv:2601.04859](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.04859)
- **AtomicRAG: Atom-Entity Graphs for Retrieval-Augmented Generation.** Hou et al., arXiv 2026 (2604.20844). Finding: stores self-contained "knowledge atoms" instead of chunks and argues that triple-based entity linking is fragile to relation-extraction errors. Reports beating RAG baselines on 5 benchmarks. Relevance: a pro-atom result, but its atoms are NL sentences, not triples. — [arXiv:2604.20844](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.20844)

### Inferences
- The positive evidence for fine units comes from *retrieval*, where precision per token matters. Our setting is *reading* a preloaded context, where the reader needs connective text and referents. Dense X's propositions are also full NL sentences, rewritten to be self-contained. A typed claim/triple layout drops even that.
- Across sources, the gains come from making units *self-contained in natural language* (decontextualized sentences, molecular facts), not from making them structured.

### Gaps
- I found no study of propositions vs verbatim sentences as a *preloaded, budgeted reader context* (CAG setting) rather than a retrieval index.
- I could not get exact per-dataset numbers from 2602.16974 beyond the search digest.

## Q3. Knowledge graphs / triples for LLM QA vs raw text

### Takeaway
The best controlled evidence (Han et al. 2025) shows triples-only GraphRAG losing badly to plain chunk RAG: NQ F1 34.3 vs 64.8. About a third of answer entities never make it into the KG, and the triples-only system abstains ("Insufficient Information"). Adding source text back recovers a lot. HippoRAG 2, TCR-QF, ToPG and LightRAG all attach passages. This lines up closely with our failure modes. One counterpoint: for *already-structured* KG facts, linearized triples beat NL verbalizations (Dai et al.).

### Cited Findings
- **RAG vs. GraphRAG: A Systematic Evaluation and Key Insights.** Han (Haoyu) et al., arXiv 2025 (2502.11371). Findings (Llama-3.1-8B): KG-based (triplets-only) GraphRAG vs RAG F1 is **34.28 vs 64.78 on NQ** and **25.02 vs 60.04 on HotpotQA**, and MultiHop-RAG accuracy is 41.24 vs 67.02. Only about 65.8% (HotpotQA) and 65.5% (NQ) of answer entities exist in the built KG. Answer-in-context rate is 39.2% vs 88.6% (HotpotQA). The triples-only system **abstains** where RAG answers. **Triplets + source text** raises NQ F1 to 50.27 and HotpotQA to 42.60, still below RAG. Community-GraphRAG is close to RAG and loses on the detail-oriented NovelQA subset (46.88 vs 55.28) and on unanswerable questions (80.07 vs 96.01). Relevance: **direct external replication of our three failure modes** (lost information, the reader abstains, detail questions suffer). — [arXiv:2502.11371](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.11371v3)
- **When to use Graphs in RAG: A Comprehensive Analysis for Graph Retrieval-Augmented Generation (GraphRAG-Bench).** Xiang et al., arXiv 2025 (2506.05690). Finding: the abstract notes that "GraphRAG frequently underperforms vanilla RAG on many real-world tasks". The benchmark separates fact retrieval from complex reasoning and summarization to find where graphs help. Relevance: graphs pay off on hierarchical, multi-hop reasoning, not simple fact retrieval, which is what RepLiQA mostly asks. — [arXiv:2506.05690](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.05690)
- **From RAG to Memory: Non-Parametric Continual Learning for Large Language Models (HippoRAG 2).** Gutiérrez et al., ICML 2025 (2502.14802). Finding: structure-augmented RAG (KG-based) "performance on more basic factual memory tasks drops considerably below standard RAG". HippoRAG 2 fixes this with **deeper passage integration**: passages become graph nodes next to phrase nodes. Relevance: the leading KG-RAG system concedes that triples need passages attached. — [arXiv:2502.14802](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.14802)
- **How to Mitigate Information Loss in Knowledge Graphs for GraphRAG: Leveraging Triple Context Restoration and Query-Driven Feedback (TCR-QF).** Huang (Manzong) et al., IJCAI 2025 (2501.15378). Finding: text-to-triple conversion loses contextual cues. Restoring each triple's source sentence (by embedding similarity) and adding missing facts gives +29.1% EM and +15.5% F1 over GraphRAG baselines on 5 QA sets. Relevance: **the closest published analogue to our "same selection rendered as verbatim sentences" fix.** — [arXiv:2501.15378](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.15378); venue at [IJCAI 2025](https://www.ijcai.org/proceedings/2025/901)
- **In-depth Analysis of Graph-based RAG in a Unified Framework.** Zhou (Yingli) et al., arXiv 2025 (2503.04338). Finding: a unified comparison of graph-RAG methods from specific to abstract QA. New variants that combine techniques win per task type. Relevance: a further controlled comparison that can be cited for "graph wins depend on question type". — [arXiv:2503.04338](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.04338)
- **From Local to Global: A Graph RAG Approach to Query-Focused Summarization (GraphRAG).** Edge et al., arXiv 2024 (2404.16130). LLM-built entity graph plus community summaries, designed for *global sense-making* questions rather than local facts. Relevance: explains why it wasn't designed for RepLiQA-style lookup. — [arXiv:2404.16130](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.16130)
- **LightRAG: Simple and Fast Retrieval-Augmented Generation.** Guo et al., arXiv 2024 (2410.05779). Dual-level graph retrieval combined with vector retrieval over chunks. Its evaluation is mostly LLM-judged comprehensiveness on summary-style queries. — [arXiv:2410.05779](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.05779)
- **NodeRAG** (Xu et al., 2504.11544) and **SiReRAG** (Zhang et al., ICLR 2025, 2412.06206) are heterogeneous-node graphs that keep text units alongside entities and relations. — [2504.11544](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.11544), [2412.06206](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.06206)
- **StructRAG: Boosting Knowledge Intensive Reasoning of LLMs via Inference-time Hybrid Information Structurization.** Li (Zhuoqun) et al., arXiv 2024 (2410.08815). A router picks the structure type (table, graph, chunk, catalogue, algorithm) per task. Relevance: no single structure is best, and choosing the format per question beats a fixed typed layout. — [arXiv:2410.08815](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.08815)
- **KAG: Boosting LLMs in Professional Domains via Knowledge Augmented Generation.** Liang et al., arXiv 2024 (2409.13731). Mutual indexing between graph and original chunks. Again, triples are kept alongside text. — [arXiv:2409.13731](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.13731)
- **Counterpoint: Large Language Models Can Better Understand Knowledge Graphs Than We Thought.** Dai et al., arXiv 2024/2025 (2402.11541). Finding: when the knowledge *is* a KG, linearized triples help fact-intensive QA more than fluent NL verbalizations. Larger LLMs are more hurt by noisy or incomplete subgraphs. Relevance: triples are not inherently unreadable. Our loss comes from *extraction* (text→triples drops facts and connectives), not from the format being illegible. — [arXiv:2402.11541](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.11541)
- **SocraticKG: Knowledge Graph Construction via QA-Driven Fact Extraction.** Choi et al., arXiv 2026 (2601.10003). Finding (snippet): direct triple extraction gives shallow coverage and fragmented subgraphs. Uses QA-driven extraction and evaluates with MINE factual recoverability. Relevance: offers a "recoverability" metric that we could apply to our claim ledger. — [arXiv:2601.10003](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.10003)

### Inferences
- The three failure modes we saw (lost connectives, dangling anaphora, reader abstains) each have a published counterpart: coverage loss and abstention (Han et al.), context-cue loss (TCR-QF), decontextualization (Molecular Facts). The literature-supported fix is "triple as index, source sentence as payload". That is the same design as our verbatim render.

### Gaps
- No paper I found tests triples vs verbatim sentences *as a fixed-budget preloaded context* (all of the above are retrieval pipelines).
- I found no RepLiQA results for any GraphRAG system.

## Q4. Compiling corpora into LLM-written notes, wikis or memory, and whether summaries lose detail

### Takeaway
Summary and memory layers (RAPTOR, MemoRAG, ReadAgent) help global and multi-hop questions *when the raw leaves stay retrievable*. 2026 "LLM wiki" work is new and mostly arXiv-only. WiCER measures catastrophic fact loss from blind compilation, and a preregistered study shows wiki-vs-RAG verdicts flip with the baseline and the judge. The memory-system literature (LongMemEval) prefers fine-grained raw values plus fact-*augmented keys* over replacing the values with facts.

### Cited Findings
- **RAPTOR: Recursive Abstractive Processing for Tree-Organized Retrieval.** Sarthi et al., ICLR 2024 (2401.18059). Finding: a tree of recursive cluster summaries; retrieving across levels gives +20% absolute on QuALITY with GPT-4. Relevance: summaries complement raw leaves (the leaves stay in the tree); they do not replace them. — [arXiv:2401.18059](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.18059)
- **MemoRAG: Boosting Long Context Processing with Global Memory-Enhanced Retrieval Augmentation.** Qian et al., WWW 2025 (2409.05591). Finding: a light long-range model builds a compressed (KV) global memory that produces draft "clues". Retrieval then fetches *raw* evidence for the expensive reader. Relevance: memory is used to *point at* source text, and the answer is grounded in verbatim retrieved spans. — [arXiv:2409.05591](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.05591)
- **A Human-Inspired Reading Agent with Gist Memory of Very Long Contexts (ReadAgent).** Lee et al., arXiv 2024 (2402.09727; ICML 2024 per the project site, unconfirmed). Gist memories plus *look-up of original pages* when details are needed. Relevance: an explicit design that admits gists lose detail. — [arXiv:2402.09727](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.09727)
- **LongMemEval: Benchmarking Chat Assistants on Long-Term Interactive Memory.** Wu (Di) et al., ICLR 2025 (2410.10813). Finding: the best memory designs use session decomposition for *value granularity* and **fact-augmented key expansion**: extracted facts help *index* the stored raw rounds. Relevance: "facts as keys, raw text as values" again, applied to memory. — [arXiv:2410.10813](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.10813)
- **Synthetic continued pretraining (EntiGraph).** Yang (Zitong) et al., arXiv 2024 (2409.07431; ICLR 2025, unconfirmed here). Rewrites a small corpus into diverse entity-relation-centric synthetic text to learn it parametrically. Relevance: here re-representation helps because the target is *weights*, not reader context. — [arXiv:2409.07431](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.07431)
- **Retrieval as Reasoning: Self-Evolving Agent-Native Retrieval via LLM-Wiki.** Ming et al., arXiv May 2026 (2605.25480). Finding: compiles documents into bidirectionally linked wiki pages that an agent searches, reads and traverses, plus an "Error Book" for self-correction. Beats HippoRAG 2, LightRAG and GraphRAG by 2.0–8.1 F1 on HotpotQA, MuSiQue and 2Wiki. The Error Book ablation costs about 3.4–4.0 F1 (per the EmergentMind digest). Relevance: an agentic, tool-accessed wiki rather than preloaded. The Error Book is a cousin of WiCER's refine loop. — [arXiv:2605.25480](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.25480); ablation via [EmergentMind](https://www.emergentmind.com/topics/llm-wiki)
- **Single-Round Vector RAG vs an LLM-Compiled Wiki: A Preregistered Comparison on a Small Multi-Domain Research Corpus.** Cochran, arXiv 2026 (2605.18490, v3). Finding: 24 papers, 13 questions. The wiki wins on cross-paper synthesis, and RAG wins on single-fact groundedness (judge-sensitive). The wiki costs about 21× more tokens per query. Judges' rank agreement on holistic groundedness is ρ=0.04 vs ρ=0.81 on concrete criteria. A v3 correction says the claimed citation-support advantage was not established against human labels. Relevance: warns that LLM-judge 1–5 scores (ours) are noisy on vague criteria and should be validated by humans. — [arXiv:2605.18490](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.18490)
- **Knowledge Compounding: An Empirical Economic Analysis of Self-Evolving Knowledge Wikis under the Agentic ROI Framework.** Wen et al., arXiv 2026 (2604.11243). An economic and cost analysis of the LLM-wiki pattern. — [arXiv:2604.11243](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.11243)
- **LLMpedia: A Transparent Framework to Materialize an LLM's Encyclopedic Knowledge at Scale.** Saeed et al., arXiv Mar 2026 (2603.24080). Finding (digest): about 741K articles generated from model memory alone. True-rate is 74.7% on Wikipedia-covered subjects and 63.2% on frontier subjects. Relevance: LLM-written knowledge is unfaithful without source grounding, which is an argument for verbatim over paraphrase. — [arXiv:2603.24080](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.24080); numbers via [EmergentMind](https://www.emergentmind.com/topics/llm-wiki)
- Practitioner context: Karpathy's "LLM wiki" (ingest/compile/query/lint) workflow, April 2026, set off a wave of open-source compilers. These are blog and GitHub sources, not research. — [DataCamp explainer](https://www.datacamp.com/blog/llm-wiki)
- Prompt-compression evidence on detail loss: **Characterizing Prompt Compression Methods for Long Context Inference** (Jha et al., Es-FoMo@ICML 2024, 2407.08892) compares extractive, abstractive and token-pruning compression. My recollection is that extractive reranker-based compression outperformed abstractive summarization and token pruning, but I did not re-read the paper to confirm. — [arXiv:2407.08892](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.08892)

### Inferences
- Every successful summary or memory design keeps a path back to verbatim source (RAPTOR leaves, MemoRAG clues→retrieval, ReadAgent look-up, LongMemEval raw values, HippoRAG 2 passages). A compiled wiki that *replaces* source text with abstractions is the configuration the literature avoids. Extractive selection rendered verbatim is the defensible middle ground.
- Our compile-noise ≥ effects problem and Cochran's judge-disagreement finding point the same way: report concrete, verifiable criteria and multiple compile seeds.

- I did not verify the Jha et al. extractive-vs-abstractive result (marked as recollection above).

## Q5. Contextual chunk enrichment (fixing dangling references)

### Takeaway
Adding document context to chunks (Anthropic contextual retrieval, late chunking, contextual document embeddings) and resolving coreference before chunking measurably improves retrieval. The 2025 coreference papers directly target our "dangling anaphora" failure. All of this evidence is about retrieval quality, not reader comprehension of a preloaded context.

### Cited Findings
- **Contextual Retrieval.** Anthropic, blog, 2024-09-19. Finding: Claude prepends a 50–100-token chunk-specific context before embedding and BM25. Top-20 retrieval failure falls 35% (contextual embeddings), 49% (+contextual BM25) and 67% (+reranking), from a 5.7% baseline. Relevance: the cheap production fix for decontextualized chunks. Our analogue is a per-claim "where/who" prefix. — [Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/news/contextual-retrieval)
- **Late Chunking: Contextual Chunk Embeddings Using Long-Context Embedding Models.** Günther et al., arXiv 2024 (2409.04701). Finding: embed the whole document, then pool per chunk, so chunk vectors carry surrounding context without training. — [arXiv:2409.04701](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.04701)
- **Reconstructing Context: Evaluating Advanced Chunking Strategies for Retrieval-Augmented Generation.** Merola & Singh, arXiv 2025 (2504.19754; 2nd Workshop on Knowledge-Enhanced IR, ECIR 2025). Finding: a head-to-head of late chunking vs contextual retrieval. Contextual retrieval preserves semantic coherence better but is more compute-heavy; late chunking is more efficient but can lose relevance (my recollection of the paper's conclusion, not re-read). — [arXiv:2504.19754](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.19754)
- **Contextual Document Embeddings.** Morris & Rush, arXiv 2024 (2410.02525; ICLR 2025, unconfirmed here). Embeddings conditioned on neighboring documents. — [arXiv:2410.02525](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.02525)
- **From Ambiguity to Accuracy: The Transformative Effect of Coreference Resolution on Retrieval-Augmented Generation systems.** Jang et al., ACL 2025 SRW (2507.07847). Finding: resolving coreference in RAG documents (gpt-4o-mini resolver) improves both retrieval and QA, and **smaller models benefit more**. Relevance: the most direct evidence for fixing dangling anaphora. This matters for a Haiku-class reader. — [arXiv:2507.07847](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.07847)
- **CLAP: Coreference-Linked Augmentation for Passage Retrieval.** Xu (Huanwei) et al., CIKM 2025 (2508.06941). Finding: chunk, resolve coreference within chunks, then generate local pseudo-queries. Up to +20.68 nDCG@10 for dense retrievers. Argues that chunking which breaks coreference chains adds noise. — [arXiv:2508.06941](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.06941)
- **Beyond Chunk-Then-Embed** (2602.16974, above): contextualized chunking helps proposition segmentation the most, consistent with propositions being the most context-starved unit. — [arXiv:2602.16974](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.16974)

### Inferences
- For a preloaded wiki, the equivalent of contextual retrieval is a *text-level* fix: rewrite each selected sentence with antecedents resolved (coreference-resolved verbatim), or prepend a short doc/section header. This is predicted to help most where our verbatim render still fails (pronoun-initial sentences). Rewrites risk paraphrase drift (see our Haiku pitfalls note), so minimal substitutions in brackets are safer than full rewrites.

### Gaps
- I found no study measuring coreference-resolved or contextualized text as *reader input* in a CAG or full-context setting. All the evidence is retrieval-side.
- I did not re-read 2504.19754 to confirm its exact conclusion (marked as recollection).
