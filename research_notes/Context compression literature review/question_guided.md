# Question-guided context selection, compression and knowledge internalization (2024–2026)

Scope: work that uses synthetic or self-generated questions (or simulated queries) to decide what to keep, how to compress, or how to represent documents for LLM QA. Relevance is judged against our setup: a query-agnostic compile of a corpus into a fixed budget, with a plan to generate about 20 questions per document and keep the answer sentences verbatim, plus a WiCER-style probe-and-refine loop.

Verification: every arXiv ID below was checked against the arXiv API on 2026-11-07, and title and first author match. Venues come from the arXiv comment field or the conference listing unless marked "(venue unverified)". Pre-2024 papers appear only as baselines and are marked as such.

## Q1. Generated or simulated queries used to decide what to keep (prompt and KV-cache compression)

### Takeaway
Nobody in this area keeps verbatim sentences because LLM-generated questions are answered by them. The nearest work splits two ways. Some methods are query-agnostic and use a proxy objective: KVzip reconstructs the context, Expected Attention predicts future queries statistically, and LLMLingua-2 is token-level. Others simulate the query at inference time (LAQ, SpecKV, TPC). The most important 2026 result is a negative one: sentence selection tuned to one question does no better than random deletion on later questions. Our "select sentences for N synthetic questions" design is open ground, but it could fail in the same way.

### Cited Findings
- **KVzip: Query-Agnostic KV Cache Compression with Context Reconstruction.** Jang-Hyun Kim et al., NeurIPS 2025 (oral). https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.23416. *Finding:* scores each KV pair by how much the LLM needs it to reconstruct the original context, then evicts the low-scoring pairs. This gives 3–4x smaller KV caches and about 2x faster decoding with negligible loss on QA, retrieval, reasoning and code. Query-aware eviction degrades even at a 90% budget in multi-query settings. *Relevance:* the strongest query-agnostic baseline. Its objective (reconstruct everything) is the opposite of ours (keep what answers likely questions), and its multi-query result supports building the compile query-agnostically. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.23416)
- **Lookahead Q-Cache: Achieving More Consistent KV Cache Eviction via Pseudo Query (LAQ).** Yixuan Wang et al., EMNLP 2025. https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.20334. *Finding:* generates a cheap pseudo lookahead response and uses its queries as the observation window for eviction. This improves LongBench by 1–4 points under limited budgets. *Relevance:* a direct analogue of "simulate the question, then keep what it attends to", applied per query at the KV level. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.20334); [ACL Anthology](https://preview.aclanthology.org/setup/2025.emnlp-main.1732)
- **Draft-based Approximate Inference for LLMs (SpecKV / SpecPC).** Kevin Galim et al., ICLR 2026. https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.08373. *Finding:* a small draft model's lookahead output and attention predict token and KV importance better than prefill attention. The paper gives theory and experiments in support of lookahead-based importance. *Relevance:* backs the idea that a model's own simulated output is a good salience signal, though it still needs the real query. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.08373)
- **Expected Attention: KV Cache Compression by Estimating Attention from Future Queries Distribution.** Alessio Devoto et al., arXiv 2025 (venue unverified). https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.00636. *Finding:* training-free. It estimates KV importance from a predicted distribution of future queries instead of observed attention. *Relevance:* query-agnostic and models "future queries" statistically rather than generating them. A useful contrast with explicit question generation. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.00636)
- **Task-agnostic Prompt Compression with Context-aware Sentence Embedding and Reward-guided Task Descriptor (TPC).** Barys Liskavets et al., arXiv 2025. https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.13374. *Finding:* a task descriptor, RL-tuned on context–query pairs, generates a context-relevant task description. Sentences are then scored for relevance to that description, with no user question. Reports SOTA on LongBench and ZeroSCROLLS. *Relevance:* the closest published pipeline to ours (generate a pseudo-query, select sentences). It uses one generated descriptor where we would use about 20 questions. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.13374)
- **Prompt Compression with Context-Aware Sentence Encoding for Fast and Improved LLM Inference (CPC).** Barys Liskavets et al., AAAI 2025. https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.01227. *Finding:* trains a sentence encoder contrastively on synthetic (question, relevant sentence, irrelevant sentence) data and keeps sentences by question relevance. Sentence-level, and up to 10.93x faster than token-level methods. *Relevance:* query-aware, but its synthetic-QA data recipe could train a "question-worthiness" sentence scorer for us. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.01227)
- **Evaluating Context Compaction on the Questions That Come Later.** Junwei Yao et al., arXiv Oct 2026. https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.27301. *Finding:* on MultiRC, keeping the sentences an LLM judges necessary for the current question gains +11.4 points on that question at 25% of tokens. On later questions, no current-question sentence selector beats random sentence deletion. Question-agnostic LLMLingua-2 beats random sentence deletion by 12.5 points on later MultiRC questions, partly because it keeps about 2x the content words. On Evidence Inference, sentence selection beats LLMLingua-2. *Relevance:* **the most important caution for us.** Selection keyed to specific questions may not transfer to unseen benchmark questions. Our pooled ~20 questions per doc should be tested against random-sentence and LLMLingua-2 baselines at equal budget. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.27301)
- **LLMLingua-2: Data Distillation for Efficient and Faithful Task-Agnostic Prompt Compression.** Zhuoshi Pan et al., Findings of ACL 2024. https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.12968. *Finding:* a token classifier distilled from GPT-4 compressions. Task-agnostic. *Relevance:* the query-agnostic token-level baseline that Yao et al. (2026) found surprisingly strong on later questions. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.12968)
- Baseline (pre-2024): **Selective Context / "Compressing Context to Enhance Inference Efficiency of Large Language Models".** Yucheng Li et al., EMNLP 2023. https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.06201. Prunes lexical units with low self-information. This is the canonical "self-information" approach. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.06201)

### Inferences
- Published methods either compress per query (LAQ, SpecKV, CPC) or compress query-agnostically with a proxy objective (KVzip, Expected Attention, LLMLingua-2, TPC). Pooling many generated questions to build one fixed query-agnostic extract sits between the two, and I found no paper that evaluates it directly.
- Yao et al. (2026) suggest that sentence-granular selection is brittle on unseen questions while token-level, content-word-preserving compression transfers better. A question-guided extract should be compared at equal token budget against (a) random sentences, (b) LLMLingua-2 and (c) our verbatim render/truncate baselines.
- KVzip's finding that query-aware eviction degrades in multi-query settings matches Yao et al. Anything over-fit to specific questions loses coverage.

### Gaps
- I found no paper that generates N questions per document and keeps the answer-bearing sentences as a query-agnostic extract, and so no measured result for that exact recipe.
- I did not verify a 2024–2026 paper that uses self-information to choose which sentences are question-worthy, as opposed to token pruning.

## Q2. Doc2Query family, hypothetical-question indexing, and whether generated questions match real question distributions

### Takeaway
Indexing documents by generated questions is mature (Doc2Query--, HyQE, QAEncoder, HyPE, Doc2Query++), and the recurring problems are hallucinated or redundant queries and low diversity. Direct evidence on distribution match says LLM questions differ systematically from human benchmark questions. They ask for longer, more descriptive answers, spread their focus evenly over the text where human questions show positional bias, cover a narrower range of question types, and concentrate on salient spans.

### Cited Findings
- Baseline (pre-2024): **Doc2Query--: When Less is More.** Mitko Gospodinov et al., ECIR 2023. https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.03266. Filtering hallucinated generated queries before indexing improves retrieval and shrinks the index. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.03266)
- **Doc2Query++: Topic-Coverage based Document Expansion and its Application to Dense Retrieval via Dual-Index Fusion.** Tzu-Lin Kuo et al., arXiv 2025. https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.09557. *Finding:* unconstrained LLM query generation gives hallucinated, redundant, low-diversity queries. Conditioning generation on topics from topic modeling plus selected keywords improves sparse and dense retrieval over Doc2Query and Doc2Query--. *Relevance:* evidence that our ~20 questions per doc need explicit coverage control (topic or keyword seeding), or they collapse onto the same spans. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.09557)
- **HyQE: Ranking Contexts with Hypothetical Query Embeddings.** Weichao Zhou et al., arXiv 2024 (venue unverified). https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.15262. *Finding:* ranks contexts by the similarity between the user query and LLM-generated hypothetical queries for each context. *Relevance:* question-to-question matching as a salience signal. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.15262)
- **QAEncoder: Towards Aligned Representation Learning in Question Answering Systems.** Zhengren Wang et al., ACL 2025 (oral). https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.20434. *Finding:* represents a document by the expected embedding of its potential queries, estimated from generated queries, plus a document fingerprint. Training-free, with no extra index storage. *Relevance:* treats "the set of questions a document answers" as the document's representation, which is the same intuition as our plan. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.20434)
- **Bridging the Question–Answer Gap in Retrieval-Augmented Generation: Hypothetical Prompt Embeddings (HyPE).** Vake, Vičič, Tošić, IEEE Access 2025. No arXiv version found. *Finding (author-reported):* generating hypothetical questions per chunk at index time gives up to +42 points context precision and +45 points claim recall over standard approaches across six datasets. *Relevance:* a practical hypothetical-question indexing method, but figures are self-reported. — [catalog record](https://library.kab.ac.ug/Record/doaj-art-b20a51ff6c854ed6927e6b35c567f094/Description); [IEEE](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/11080443)
- **Can LLMs Ask Good Questions?** Yueheng Zhang et al., arXiv 2025. https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.03491. *Finding:* compared with human-authored questions (TriviaQA/HotpotQA per a secondary summary), LLM questions from context demand longer descriptive answers and spread their focus more evenly across the context. Human QA questions show positional bias. *Relevance:* direct evidence of a distribution mismatch. RepLiQA questions are human-written with short answers, so LLM questions may target different sentences. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.03491); [alphaXiv summary](https://alphaxiv.org/overview/2501.03491v2)
- **How Do LLMs Ask Questions? A Pragmatic Comparison with Human Question-Asking.** Jung et al., NeurIPS 2025 (arXiv ID not verified). *Finding (from listing/summary):* LLM questions cover a narrower set of question types than human Reddit questions, and prompting introduces further biases. — [NeurIPS listing](https://neurips.cc/virtual/2025/129764)
- **Synthetic Test Collections for Retrieval Evaluation.** Hossein A. Rahmani et al., SIGIR 2024. https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.07767. *Finding:* LLM-generated queries (and judgments) can be used to build IR test collections. *Relevance:* a supportive data point for synthetic queries as stand-ins for real ones, in IR rather than reading-comprehension QA. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.07767)
- **Self-Study Reconsidered: The Hidden Fragility of Learning from Self-Generated QA.** Ekaterina Alimaskina et al., arXiv Jun 2026. https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.32002. *Finding:* question generation is "an implicit policy". Coverage saturates early, questions concentrate on salient spans, diverse prompts converge on the same regions, and local presentation (even leftover markup) can hijack it. Tying each question to a fixed target span reduces the selection bias. Filtering instruction-like spans before answering cuts injection compliance from 88% to 13%. *Relevance:* **directly predicts a failure mode of our plan.** Twenty free-form questions per doc will cluster. Assigning target sentences or sections before generating would counter that. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.32002)

### Inferences
- Two independent results (Zhang et al. 2025; Alimaskina et al. 2026) say generated questions are not a uniform or human-like sample of what a document can answer. Combined with our measured detail-level held-out failures (memory: held-out displacement), coverage control should matter more than question count.
- Doc2Query++ (topic and keyword seeding) and Self-Study Reconsidered (tie each question to a fixed target) both point to the same fix: seed generation by position or topic rather than asking for "20 questions".

### Gaps
- I found no study that measures overlap between LLM-generated questions and RepLiQA's human questions specifically (e.g., the fraction of gold answer sentences hit by generated questions). We can measure this ourselves cheaply.
- I did not confirm an arXiv ID for "How Do LLMs Ask Questions?" (NeurIPS 2025).

## Q3. Self-generated probes for distilling knowledge into memory or weights (closest competitors)

### Takeaway
A dense 2024–2026 line trains corpus knowledge into weights, LoRAs or trained KV caches from synthetic, self-generated question or conversation data: EntiGraph, Self-Tuning, prompt distillation, Knowledge-Instruct, Knowledge Modules, Cartridges/self-study, SEAL, Active Reading, Doc-to-LoRA, MixSD. They consistently find that plain next-token training on the raw text is weak, and that synthetic QA or context distillation is what works. None of them outputs a human-readable, verbatim, fixed-budget text context like ours. That is our point of difference, and also our main competitive risk on accuracy per token.

### Cited Findings
- **Synthetic continued pretraining (EntiGraph).** Zitong Yang et al., ICLR 2025. https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.07431. *Finding:* extracts entities from a small corpus and generates text about entity relations, then continues pretraining on it. QA accuracy scales log-linearly with synthetic tokens and is complementary to RAG. *Relevance:* the canonical "synthesize to internalize" competitor. Weights, not context. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.07431)
- **Instruction-tuned Language Models are Better Knowledge Learners (pre-instruction-tuning).** Zhengbao Jiang et al., ACL 2024. https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.12847. *Finding:* training on QA pairs before continued pretraining on documents improves later fact recall. *Relevance:* evidence that the QA format is what makes knowledge retrievable. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.12847)
- **Self-Tuning: Instructing LLMs to Effectively Acquire New Knowledge through Self-Teaching.** Xiaoying Zhang et al., 2024 (venue unverified). https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.06326. *Finding:* the model generates its own memorization, comprehension and self-reflection tasks over new documents (self-teaching) and learns the knowledge better than continued pretraining. *Relevance:* a self-generated-probe recipe close to WiCER's refine loop. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.06326)
- **Efficient Knowledge Injection in LLMs via Self-Distillation (prompt distillation).** Kalle Kujanpää et al., arXiv 2024 (v2 2025). https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.14964. *Finding:* the model generates QA pairs about new facts, and a LoRA student matches a teacher that sees the facts in its prompt, with no larger teacher. *Relevance:* self-generated questions as the distillation carrier. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.14964)
- **Knowledge-Instruct: Effective Continual Pre-training from Limited Data using Instructions.** Oded Ovadia et al., arXiv 2025. https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.05571. *Finding:* information-dense synthetic instruction data injects knowledge from small corpora with less forgetting than CPT. Introduces the "Companies" benchmark. *Relevance:* competitor. Does not evaluate on RepLiQA. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.05571)
- **Training Plug-n-Play Knowledge Modules with Deep Context Distillation.** Lucas Caccia et al., COLM 2025. https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.08727. *Finding:* per-document LoRA "knowledge modules" trained to match the hidden states and logits of a teacher with the document in context beat next-token training and pre-instruction tuning, and work alongside RAG. *Relevance:* a per-document memory unit, comparable to our per-document extract. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.08727)
- **Cartridges: Lightweight and general-purpose long context representations via self-study.** Sabri Eyuboglu et al., arXiv 2025 (an icml.cc 2025 listing exists, likely a workshop; main venue unverified). https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.06266. *Finding:* trains a small KV cache per corpus. Naive next-token training does not compete with in-context learning. "Self-study" (synthetic conversations about corpus chunks plus context distillation) matches ICL with 38.6x less memory and 26.4x higher throughput, extends effective context (128k to 484k on MTOB), and the caches compose without retraining. *Relevance:* **the closest competitor.** Same goal (a fixed-size, query-agnostic corpus representation built offline from self-generated questions), but the output is opaque trained KV rather than verbatim text. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.06266); [project page](https://scalingintelligence.stanford.edu/pubs/cartridges)
- **Learned Structure in Cartridges: Keys as Shareable Routers in Self-Studied Representations.** Maurizio Diaz et al., arXiv 2025. https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.17032. *Finding:* Cartridge keys act as stable, shareable retrieval routers and most compression lives in the values. Sampled-chunk initialization speeds convergence. *Relevance:* mechanistic follow-up. Initializing from sampled corpus chunks parallels our verbatim-sentence selection. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.17032)
- **Self-Adapting Language Models (SEAL).** Adam Zweiger et al., 2025 (venue unverified). https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.10943. *Finding:* the model generates its own "self-edits" (e.g., implications of a passage) as fine-tuning data, and RL optimizes the generator for downstream QA after the update. *Relevance:* learns *what to generate* from downstream QA reward. This is the trained analogue of WiCER's probe loop. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.10943)
- **Learning Facts at Scale with Active Reading.** Jessy Lin et al., arXiv 2025. https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.09494. *Finding:* the model writes its own study strategies over source documents and trains on the results. 66% on a Wikipedia-grounded SimpleQA subset (+313% relative over vanilla finetuning) and 26% on FinanceBench (+160%). Scales to a 1T-token WikiExpert-8B. *Relevance:* strong evidence that self-generated, diverse study material beats raw text. Weights, not context. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.09494)
- **Doc-to-LoRA: Learning to Instantly Internalize Contexts.** Rujikorn Charakorn et al., ICML 2026 (PMLR v306). https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.15902. *Finding:* a hypernetwork meta-learns approximate context distillation and maps a document to a LoRA in one forward pass, cutting KV memory and latency for later queries. *Relevance:* amortizes away the per-corpus training cost that self-study and Cartridges pay. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.15902); [PMLR](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v306/charakorn26a.html)
- **The Override Gap: A Magnitude Account of Knowledge Conflict Failure in Hypernetwork-Based Instant LLM Adaptation.** Shuaizhi Cheng et al., arXiv 2026. https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.23750. *Finding:* Doc-to-LoRA fails on documents that contradict pretraining, dropping to 46.4% on the deepest facts. Accuracy falls from 68% on weak-prior facts to 16% on strong-prior facts. *Relevance:* weight-based internalization is weak exactly where verbatim in-context text is strong (counterfactual or novel facts such as RepLiQA's fictional content). This is an argument for our approach. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.23750)
- **MixSD: Mixed Contextual Self-Distillation for Knowledge Injection.** Jiarui Liu et al., arXiv May 2026. https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.16865. *Finding:* mixes tokens from the model's "fact in context" and "no fact" conditionals to build supervision. Retains up to 100% of held-out capability where SFT retains as little as 1%. *Relevance:* addresses forgetting in the weight-based competitors. Not directly applicable to us. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.16865)
- **Self-Study Reconsidered** (see Q2), arXiv 2026. https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.32002. A direct critique of the self-study and synthetic-QA pipelines above: biased coverage, and prompt-injection compliance when answering. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.32002)

### Inferences
- The consistent result across EntiGraph, Knowledge Modules, Cartridges and Active Reading is that raw-text exposure underperforms synthetic, question-shaped exposure. That supports using generated questions as the selection signal. Our variant spends the questions on choosing text instead of training on it.
- Weight-based and KV-based methods are vulnerable on counterfactual or novel facts (Override Gap) and are opaque. Verbatim extracts keep exact spans, which suits RepLiQA's fictional entities and our measured finding that verbatim rendering beats truncation (memory: TACT milestones).
- Cartridges is the natural head-to-head comparison: same memory budget, same self-generated questions, text extract vs. trained KV.

### Gaps
- None of these papers evaluates on RepLiQA (checked Cartridges, Knowledge-Instruct and Knowledge Modules abstracts only; full experiment sections not checked).
- I did not confirm venues for SEAL or Self-Tuning.

## Q4. Learning to select: question-worthiness and salience prediction

### Takeaway
Little recent work predicts "which sentences will be asked about" for LLM-era QA. The best 2024 anchor is QSalience (inquisitive-question salience). Sentence-level question-relevance scorers trained on synthetic QA (CPC) and query-free task descriptors (TPC) are the closest reusable machinery.

### Cited Findings
- **Which questions should I answer? Salience Prediction of Inquisitive Questions (QSalience).** Yating Wu et al., EMNLP 2024. https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.10917. *Finding:* an instruction-tuned salience predictor trained on 1,766 linguist-annotated (context, question) pairs. Highly salient questions are more likely to be answered later in the same article, and summaries that answer more salient questions score higher. *Relevance:* a way to rank or filter our ~20 generated questions and keep the sentences behind salient ones. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.10917); [NSF PAR](https://par.nsf.gov/biblio/10631822-which-questions-should-answer-salience-prediction-inquisitive-questions)
- **SGCM: Salience-Guided Context Modeling for Question Generation.** Ding, Hong, Yao, LREC-COLING 2024 (no arXiv found). *Finding:* an auxiliary salient-sentence identification task improves paragraph-level QG. *Relevance:* salient-sentence detection as an explicit learning target. — [ACL Anthology](https://preview.aclanthology.org/setup/2024.lrec-main.1285)
- **CPC** (AAAI 2025, https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.01227) and **TPC** (arXiv 2025, https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.13374), see Q1. These are trained sentence scorers built on synthetic question–sentence pairs. TPC removes the need for a user question. — [CPC](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.01227); [TPC](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.13374)
- **Can LLMs Ask Good Questions?** (Q2): human QA questions show positional bias, while LLM questions spread evenly. This is a data point on where benchmark questions land within a document. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.03491)
- **Self-Study Reconsidered** (Q2): LLM question-worthiness is driven by local presentation and saturates early on salient spans. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.32002)

### Inferences
- A cheap, testable signal: across documents, how often does each sentence get hit by generated questions, and does hit count predict whether RepLiQA's gold questions target it? If human questions favour early positions (Zhang et al.), a positional prior may compete with LLM-question coverage.

### Gaps
- I found no 2025–2026 answer-span or question-worthiness predictor evaluated as a context-selection policy for LLM QA.

## Q5. RepLiQA and how it has been used since

### Takeaway
RepLiQA (NeurIPS 2024 Datasets & Benchmarks) has about 35 citations on Semantic Scholar. It is mostly used as an "unseen reference content" QA set for RAG, answerability, citation and auditing work. WiCER (2605.07068), the paper our project reproduces, is among the citers. I found no paper that uses RepLiQA to evaluate question-guided compression, Cartridges-style self-study or synthetic CPT.

### Cited Findings
- **RepLiQA: A Question-Answering Dataset for Benchmarking LLMs on Unseen Reference Content.** Joao Monteiro et al., NeurIPS 2024 Datasets & Benchmarks. https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.11811. *Finding:* human-written fictional reference documents with 5 QA pairs each. Models do much better on TriviaQA than RepLiQA, so they lean on memorized knowledge. Mistral Large was best and had one of the smallest recall gaps. Splits are released over time. *Relevance:* our benchmark. Fictional content blocks parametric shortcuts, so compression quality is measured directly. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.11811); [NeurIPS](https://nips.cc/virtual/2024/poster/97851); [ServiceNow blog](https://www.servicenow.com/blogs/2024/repliqa-more-robust-benchmark-qa)
- Citation list retrieved from Semantic Scholar on 2026-11-07, about 35 unique papers. — [S2 API](https://api.semanticscholar.org/graph/v1/paper/arXiv:2406.11811/citations)
- **WiCER: Wiki-memory Compile, Evaluate, Refine — Iterative Knowledge Compilation for LLM Wiki Systems.** Juan M. Huerta, arXiv May 2026. https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.07068. Cites RepLiQA. This is the paper our project builds on. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.07068)
- **Cite Pretrain: Retrieval-Free Knowledge Attribution for Large Language Models.** Yukun Huang et al., arXiv 2025. Semantic Scholar lists the venue as ICLR 2025, which conflicts with the June 2025 arXiv date, so treat the venue as unverified. https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.17585. *Usage:* RepliQA is one of the CitePretrainBench corpora, the "unseen, post-cutoff" one. The paper continually pretrains on the documents and trains the model to cite them. "Active Indexing" uses synthetic restatements of facts and gains up to 30.2 points of citation precision over passive indexing. *Relevance:* the closest RepLiQA use to ours. Synthetic data about RepLiQA documents is used to internalize them. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.17585)
- **Highlight & Summarize: RAG without the jailbreaks.** Giovanni Cherubin et al., arXiv 2025. https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.02872. *Usage:* RepliQA and BioASQ as QA evaluation. A highlighter extracts passages relevant to the question and a summarizer that never sees the question writes the answer. "Two Steps" H&S scores 95% correctness on RepliQA vs 94% for vanilla RAG. *Relevance:* evidence that extracted verbatim passages are enough for RepLiQA answers. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.02872)
- **Detecting (Un)answerability in Large Language Models with Linear Directions.** Maor Juliet Lavi et al., arXiv 2025 (S2 lists EACL; unverified). https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.22449. *Usage:* RepLiQA is one of four extractive QA datasets for unanswerability detection, alongside SQuAD 2.0, NQ and MuSiQue. *Relevance:* RepLiQA's unanswerable fraction is a known evaluation axis (memory: about 20% unanswerable). — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.22449)
- **You Only Pass Once: Answering and Abstaining Together in a Single Forward Pass of a Frozen Language Model.** Ziyang Luo et al., arXiv 2026. https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.14465. *Usage:* RepLiQA is one of the answerability-labelled sets. ### Inferences
- RepLiQA's main follow-on use is as a contamination-free, answerability-labelled QA set. That makes a question-guided compile evaluated on RepLiQA, against KVzip, Cartridges and LLMLingua-2 baselines, fairly novel.
- H&S's 95% correctness from question-conditioned extracts gives a rough ceiling for "extract then answer" on RepLiQA when the question is known. The gap between that and our query-agnostic extract measures what pre-selection costs.
